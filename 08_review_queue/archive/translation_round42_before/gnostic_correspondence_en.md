# A Gnosticism Correspondence Model for Blue Archive: Characters, Divinity, and Cosmic Order

## Abstract

This paper interprets the characters and world structure of *Blue Archive* through the lens of Gnosticism cosmology.

In many religious and mythological traditions, human existence in the here-and-now is rarely accorded self-sufficient value. Within such cosmologies, the material created realm is frequently regarded as inherently deficient, while bodily existence is seen as merely a temporary state of entrapment or oblivion; the joys, sorrows, and trivialities of everyday secular life are universally subordinated as a transitional phase that must be transcended.
Under this logic, the ultimate meaning of life is anchored entirely in "return"—only by awakening one's innate spiritual nature, casting off the shackles of the material world, and returning to the transcendent primordial source can the individual attain salvation and fulfill life's true completeness and liberation.
Yet the answer *Blue Archive* offers through this archetype thoroughly subverts this premise: while divine origins may explain "where the soul came from," they must never dictate "what one lives for." Even if individuals carry a divine origin within them, living one's life is never contingent upon first solving grandiose existential puzzles. Before embracing the life right before them, people do not need to interrogate their ontological origins, shoulder preordained cosmic missions, or discover an abstract "meaning of life."
Compared to higher dimensions that we can never comprehend—and have no need to comprehend—the "daily life" lived in day-to-day "ordinariness"—the "process itself" of laughing, making mistakes, and growing alongside companions—is what truly warrants our cherishing. Having arrived in this world, one should not invert priorities by obsessing over what came before birth or what lies after departure. Living this present life earnestly is what matters above all.
Furthermore, so-called "miracles" rarely stem from supernatural powers that violate normal reality; rather, they are born from the warmth radiated when ordinary people guard one another and make free choices within their fragile, everyday lives. Refusing to let divine essence usurp human existence is precisely what preserves this authentic, shining daily miracle.

**Keywords** Blue Archive; Gnosticism; Sophia; Yaldabaoth; Archons; Luminous Epinoia; Divine Spark; Pleroma

*Readers unfamiliar with these intricate concepts are encouraged to consult [Glossary: Gnosticism Terms and Literature](GNOSTIC_GLOSSARY_en.md).*

## I. Basic Structure of the Model

Key narrative elements in *Blue Archive*—the President's error and Sensei's arrival; the ancient order left by the Nameless Gods and their Priests; the students referred to as gods; oblivion; halos; and the demand to return to the origin in the Decagrammaton Arc—can be synthesized into a coherent whole through the framework of Gnosticism.

The correspondences adopted in this paper are as follows:

| In-Game Entity | Gnosticism Correspondence | Core Relational Correspondence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General Student Council President | Sophia | Error, repentance, withdrawal, and remedy |
| Sensei | Luminous Epinoia | Introduced after cosmic imbalance occurs |
| Essence of Nameless Gods & Old God System | Yaldabaoth-related archetype; functions as divine source | Ancient divine rule, world order, and source of divine power |
| Nameless Priests | Archons, inheriting aspects of Yaldabaoth | Delegated divine rule, enforcing missions, governing and restructuring the world |
| Forgotten Gods / Students | Pleromatic divine entities and their earthly manifestations | Divine origin, oblivion, and individualized living |
| Halo | External manifestation of the Divine Spark | Connection between innate divinity and individual survival |

This correspondence model is grounded primarily in the *Apocryphon of John*, supplemented by Valentinian sources.

## II. General Student Council President = Sophia

### 2.1 Error, Repentance, and Remedy

The correspondence between the General Student Council President and Sophia is manifest above all at the narrative origin of the entire work. In the *Apocryphon of John*, Sophia acted autonomously without her partner (Syzygy) and without the consent of the primordial source, thereby precipitating an ontological imbalance across the cosmos. Upon recognizing her error, she fell into profound repentance, initiating an enduring process of rectification and remedy. *Blue Archive* similarly anchors the very genesis of its plot in the President's "mistake" (*miss*) and remorse, a core monologue reiterated verbatim in the Final Chapter:

> **連邦生徒会長**: ……私のミスでした。
>
> English: "...It was my mistake."[^1]

> **連邦生徒会長**: 私の選択、そしてそれによって招かれたこの全ての状況。
>
> English: "My choices, and all of these circumstances brought about by them."[^2]

> **連邦生徒会長**: 結局、この結果にたどり着いて初めて、あなたの方が正しかったことを悟るだなんて……。
>
> English: "To think that only after arriving at this outcome do I finally realize you were right..."[^3]

What the President acknowledges is not merely a loss of control, but an explicit attribution of this cataclysm to "her own choices"; and the first step toward restitution was acknowledging the correctness of Sensei's original judgment. In the Prologue, she entrusted the hope of reversing this doomed trajectory to Sensei:

> **？？？**: 私が信じられる大人である、あなたになら、
>
> English: "If it is you, an adult I can trust,"[^4]

> **？？？**: この捻じれて歪んだ先の終着点とは、また別の結果を……。
>
> English: "Perhaps a different outcome from this twisted, distorted destination..."[^5]

> **？？？**: そこへ繋がる選択肢は……きっと見つかるはずです。
>
> English: "The choices leading there... you will surely be able to find them."[^6]

Although the President's marital or consort status is never addressed in the text, her solitary decision-making and unilateral assumption of responsibility culminating in crisis closely mirror Sophia's narrative function—recognizing her own fault and summoning an external helper to rectify the flaw.[^7]

### 2.2 The "Youngest" Status as Structural Corroboration

Another telling detail in the President's characterization emerges in Part 2 Prologue, where the circumstances of her inauguration are revealed:

> **アオイ**: これは……2年前かしら。
> 連邦生徒会長が就任した頃の写真ね。
>
> English: "Is this... from two years ago? A photo from around the time the General Student Council President took office."[^8]

> **アユム**: 史上最年少での就任、と当時話題になりましたよね。
>
> English: "Her appointment as the youngest in history was quite the topic back then, wasn't it?"[^9]

This historical attribute of being the "youngest" corresponds precisely to Sophia's genealogical status in Valentinian emanation theology. In *Against Heresies*, Irenaeus records that Sophia:

> "was the youngest of the Duodecad"[^10]

Within the hierarchy of divine emanations, the symbol of the "youngest" is no trivial numerical coincidence; it signifies an entity positioned at the outermost perimeter of the Pleromatic order, and consequently the one most susceptible to turbulence and rupture. In *Blue Archive*, her inauguration as the youngest in history serves as substantial corroborative evidence mapping her to the mythic prototype of Sophia.

### 2.3 Disappearance and Continued Presence

At the story's inception, the President is already missing. However, in Chapter 4, Episode 12 of the Final Chapter, when Plana completes her calculations inside the Shittim Chest, a critical revelation occurs:

> **プラナ**: シッテムの箱のOS、A.R.O.N.Aである私と、アロナ先輩……
> なぜ二人が似て非なる存在であるのか……理解しました。
>
> English: "Between Arona-senpai and myself, A.R.O.N.A., the OS of the Shittim Chest... I now understand why the two of us are similar yet distinct existences."[^11]

> **プラナ**: ここに居たのですね……だからこそ、誰にも見つけられなかった。
> そうでしょう？
>
> English: "You were here all along... That is why no one could find you. Isn't that right?"[^12]

> **プラナ**: ――――連邦生徒会長。
>
> English: "——General Student Council President."[^13]

This revelation reframes the President's "disappearance" not as mere absence, but as a transmutation of location and mode of being. While she no longer governs publicly, she remains omnipresent within the deep interior of the narrative via Arona and the Shittim Chest.
In the *Apocryphon of John*, following her transgression, Sophia does not return to the upper heights nor fall irrevocably into the abyss; instead, she is placed in the "Ninth Sphere" (the intermediate realm above the seven heavens of the Archons) to await and oversee the restoration of deficiency. This theological positioning parallels the President's hidden vigil inside the OS.[^14]

## III. Sensei = Luminous Epinoia

### 3.1 The Helper Function in the Primary Texts

In the *Apocryphon of John*, immediately after the divine power of Sophia enters into Adam, the celestial realm dispatches a crucial savior to him. The text explicitly refers to this presence as:

> “a helper to Adam, luminous Epinoia”[^15]

The core mission of the Luminous Epinoia is to awaken the created individual, guide him to recognize his inner divine source, and thereby escape the absolute shackles of material entrapment. She resides secretly within Adam, impenetrable and uncontrollable by the Archons, participating directly in the progressive rectification of the Mother's deficiency. This archetype bridges two structural tiers: looking upward, it connects to Sophia's initial imbalance and overarching cosmic remedy; looking downward, it guards the living individual from being utterly determined by the cold decrees of lower rulers.

### 3.2 The President's Entrustment and Sensei's Action

In *Blue Archive*, Sensei is entrusted by the General Student Council President to arrive in Kivotos, helping students overturn what would otherwise be catastrophic ends. This positions Sensei in direct correspondence with the Luminous Epinoia. Sensei tangibly fulfills this essential role of the helper.
In the parallel timeline, when the Priests attempt to dictate Shiroko's fate purely through her divine nature, Sensei refuses to accept their predetermination. The Priests declare:

> **無名の司祭B**：アレは、お前の知る生徒ではない！！
>
> English: "That is not a student you know!!"[^16]

> **無名の司祭C**：アレは神が顕現したもの――「神秘」であり、「恐怖」であり、「崇高」……「光」であり、「絶対者」！！
>
> English: "That is a god manifested—it is 'Mystery,' it is 'Terror,' it is 'Sublime'...'Light,' and 'the Absolute'!!"[^17]

To which Sensei replies:

> **先生**：私の「世界」で苦しんでいる、ただの「子ども」だよ。
>
> English: "She is just a child suffering in my 'world.'"[^18]

Both sides look upon the exact same person. While the Priests reduce her to divine authority and utility, Sensei cares solely for the student currently enduring suffering.

Here, a potential contradiction warrants clarification: if the mission of the Luminous Epinoia is to guide humanity to recognize its divine origin and "return to the primordial state of fullness," does this clash with Sensei's defense of students' secular everyday lives?
A crucial distinction must be drawn: our critique is not directed against the beauty of the primordial source itself, but against the inverted logic that obsesses over transcendent origins while devaluing living reality. In truth, the most essential goodness does not require abandoning life to be attained; rather, it is when people earnestly engage in ordinary daily life, confront choices, and experience companionship that they can genuinely perceive these essential qualities in tranquility.

Thus, Sensei preserves the functional core of the Luminous Epinoia—guiding and aiding individuals in the wake of cosmic imbalance. The game translates this aid into safeguarding students' growth and autonomous choices in the present world. Through this transformation, "completeness" is restored to the living experience of humanity. The functional parallel holds, while the narrative destination of salvation has been rewritten.

## IV. Nameless Gods and Yaldabaoth: Ancient Divine Power and Functional Division

### 4.1 Masters of the World Prior to the Existing Order

In the Final Chapter, Gematria debates the Nameless Priests and their legacy. Maestro first outlines their historical standing, after which Black Suit explains the relationship between the gods and the priests:

> **マエストロ**：そうだな……彼らは端的に言うなら、キヴォトス以前に存在していたこの世界の主のことだ。
>
> English: "Indeed... Simply put, they are the masters of this world who existed prior to Kivotos."[^19]

> **黒服**：「名もなき神」とそれを崇拝する「無名の司祭」――
> 彼らはキヴォトスの神秘の下に堆積し、痕跡だけが残るはずだった存在……
>
> English: "'The Nameless Gods' and the 'Nameless Priests' who worship them—they were beings meant to remain only as traces deposited beneath the mysteries of Kivotos..."[^20]

> **黒服**：「名もなき神」――それは大地、海原、天災といった……所謂、太古の昔より存在する「神秘」や「恐怖」――とでも申しましょうか。
> 彼らは自然を模った形で顕現するとされております。
>
> English: "'The Nameless Gods'—be it the earth, the ocean, or natural disasters... one could say they are 'Mysteries' or 'Terrors' existing since primordial antiquity. It is said they manifest in forms imitating nature."[^21]

As shown here, the Nameless Gods constituted the ancient ruling order predating current Kivotos, while the Nameless Priests serve as the stewards and restorationists of this former order.

This system corresponds to Yaldabaoth in the *Apocryphon of John*. The text's Yaldabaoth similarly exercises organizing and governing dominion over the lower cosmos, proclaiming himself the sole god in ignorance of higher realms, and establishing an intricate network of control through subordinate Archons.[^7]

### 4.2 Material Restructuring and the Rewriting of the World

In the narrative, the power of the Nameless Gods is not mere abstract myth, but is concrete physical capability capable of altering reality:

> **ケイ**：「名もなき神」の力の中で代表的なのは、物質の再構築です。
>
> English: "Among the powers of the 'Nameless Gods,' the most representative is the restructuring of matter."[^22]

> **ケイ**：はい。「名もなき神」の力は、波動と粒子の関係を根本から変化させることができます。
>
> English: "Yes. The power of the 'Nameless Gods' can fundamentally alter the relationship between waves and particles."[^23]

This ability to restructure matter directly and rewrite baseline physical laws endows the Nameless Gods system with distinct "Demiurge" qualities—it represents both ancient sovereign power and a technological base designed to reformat the world. The essence of the Nameless Gods corroborates this directly when explaining Aris's original purpose:

> **柔らかい声**：現代の言葉を借りれば、彼方はある種の「中継器」であり「増幅器」でした。
>
> English: "In modern terms, you were a sort of 'repeater' and 'amplifier.'"[^24]

> **柔らかい声**：あの司祭たちによって、彼方は名もなき神々の王女として準備されました。
>
> English: "You were prepared by those priests as the princess of the Nameless Gods."[^25]

> **柔らかい声**：今の世界を、名もなき神々が本来の姿で統治する世界に戻すために。
>
> English: "In order to return the present world to one ruled by the Nameless Gods in their original form."[^26]

> **柔らかい声**：名もなき神々の王女を稼働させるための鍵。
> 司祭たちの道具として、王女に役割を果たさせる装置でした。
>
> English: "The key to activating the princess of the Nameless Gods. A device to make the princess fulfill her role as a tool of the priests."[^27]

The Priests manufactured both princess and key, completely instrumentalizing life and reducing it strictly to mechanical functions for resetting reality. This mirrors Yaldabaoth's Demiurgic logic: an entity's existential value is equated entirely with its assigned utility.

In the *Apocryphon of John* (NHC II,1 15,1–19,10), Yaldabaoth and the Archons craft the human body by assigning each organ and faculty a rigid function designed to serve the material order, attempting to bind humanity into an enslaved vessel; they subsequently create *Heimarmene* (Fate):

> “For they are bound with measures and times and moments, since it (fate) is lord over everything.”

(NHC II,1 28,30–32).

The Priests' designation of princess and key as instruments to carry out a predetermined mission closely parallels this structure of subjugating life to a lower cosmic order through Heimarmene.

### 4.3 Essence, Priests, and Archetypal Division

This paper maintains the connection between the essence of the Nameless Gods and Yaldabaoth, while distributing active enforcement functions to the Priests. This approach accommodates both the divine sovereignty and demiurgic qualities of the ancient system while accounting for the sharp divergence in attitude between the essence and its executors.

Ancient accounts of the Demiurge themselves exhibited varied facets. Ptolemy's *Letter to Flora* places the lawgiver-creator between the supreme perfect source and the adversary, interpreting him as an equitable intermediary. While differing from the ignorant, envious depiction in the *Apocryphon of John*, it demonstrates that demiurgic and governing functions could be repositioned across theological frameworks.[^28]

Thus, the magnanimous temperament of the Nameless Gods' essence does not invalidate the Yaldabaoth correspondence. The game allows the essence to retain primordial sovereignty and source functions, while assigning dogmatic exclusion, coercion, and cosmic reformatting to the Priests. Detailed analysis follows in subsequent sections.

## V. Nameless Priests = Archons, Inheriting Aspects of Yaldabaoth

### 5.1 Worship Relationships and Delegated Authority

The Priests describe their power with absolute clarity:

> **無名の司祭A**：司祭は神を崇めるがゆえ、「崇高」を所有できる。
>
> English: "Because priests revere the gods, they can possess the 'Sublime.'"[^29]

Their authority rests upon their relationship of worship. Though not the supreme origin themselves, they intervene using divine authority. This aligns the Nameless Priests with the Archons: serving as lower-world proxies who decree laws in the name of the divine and exercise dominion over entities within their jurisdiction.

Furthermore, in the *Apocryphon of John* (NHC II,1 11,22–12,10), Yaldabaoth establishes seven rulers to govern seven planetary heavens. The Nameless Priests likewise appear as seven distinct entities, designated A through G.

### 5.2 World Restoration

Key summarizes the original design to Ohr:

> **ケイ**：今のテクスチャーを否定し、アリスを楔石としたあと……。
>
> English: "After negating the present Texture and using Aris as the keystone..."[^30]

> **ケイ**：すべての分化された神聖を解体し、始まる前の世界に戻る。
> そういうことでしょう？
>
> English: "Disassemble all differentiated divinity and return to the world before the beginning. That is what this is, right?"[^31]

> **オウル**：目的が何であれ、あなたは本来そういう存在なのでは？
> 名もなき神々の力の残滓は、そのために存在するのでしょう？
>
> English: "Whatever the objective, aren't you originally that kind of existence? Isn't the remnant of the Nameless Gods' power meant for that?"[^32]

The Priests' ambition goes far beyond reclaiming secular dominion. They seek to invalidate the present reality, dissolve all subsequently differentiated manifestations of divinity, and roll reality back to the state before the beginning. In their eyes, the current world and its living forms are illegitimate aberrations from ancient liturgy—objects that must be thoroughly annulled, purged, and undone.

## VI. Alternative Possibilities for the Essence of the Nameless Gods

### 6.1 Potential Tension

The correspondence between the essence of the Nameless Gods and Yaldabaoth does not exhaust every function it carries within the drama—in short, it is difficult to map them one-to-one. After all, the game likely referenced the Gnosticism system rather than mechanically replicating it. Yet this very divergence opens room for theoretical branching.

The "Soft Voice" describes the power of the Nameless Gods as a more universal, immanent strength:

> **柔らかい声**：真の姿に還り、全知全能の力のもと、数多の事を成し遂げることができます。
>
> English: "By returning to your true form, under omniscient and omnipotent power, you can accomplish many things."[^35]

> **柔らかい声**：名もなき神々の力とは本来、「可能性の力」を意味しているのですから。
>
> English: "Because the power of the Nameless Gods originally meant the 'power of possibility.'"[^36]

> **柔らかい声**：だからそれは、さほど特別なものではありません。
>
> English: "Therefore, it is not something particularly exceptional."[^37]

> **柔らかい声**：今を生きる誰しもが、少しずつ己の中に秘めている力でもあります。
>
> English: "It is also a power that everyone living in the present secretly harbors a little of within themselves."[^38]

This shows that the power of the Nameless Gods is not confined to ancient weaponry dormant in specific ruins; it connects directly to the latent potential residing within every living person. The essence of the Nameless Gods thus acquires source attributes: its power exists universally, in minute measures, within concrete individuals.

This closely parallels the notion of the Divine Spark. Consequently, as an alternative theoretical branch, one could group the essence of the Nameless Gods alongside the President (Sophia) and the students (contemporary manifestations of the Forgotten Gods) into the Pleromatic divine spectrum. Here, Pleroma denotes the comprehensive realm of divine origin rather than a rigid hierarchy; all three share the same divine background while fulfilling distinct narrative functions within the mundane order.

This might appear at odds with the earlier correspondence between the Nameless Gods and Yaldabaoth, as Yaldabaoth carries an overwhelmingly negative connotation. Yet this serves merely as a theoretical branch: the broader system of elements associated with the Nameless Gods remains fundamentally aligned with Yaldabaoth. What is brought into the Pleroma side here is strictly that "Soft Voice" that converses with Aris. While the text does not explicitly identify this voice as the essence of the Nameless Gods, it is functionally equivalent; this paper treats that soft voice consistently as the essence of the Nameless Gods.

### 6.2 The God Accepts the Choice; The Priests Cling to the Mission

When Aris declares her choice, the Voice peacefully accepts her trial:

> **柔らかい声**：……分かりました。やりたいようにやってごらんなさい。
> そして、私にも見せてください。
>
> English: "...I understand. Try doing as you wish. And let me see it as well."[^39]

> **柔らかい声**：追従する者たちが、彼方を永遠の裏切り者だと呪うことになったとしても。
>
> English: "Even if your followers end up cursing you as an eternal traitor."[^40]

> **柔らかい声**：私はこのすべての過程を、心から楽しみました。
>
> English: "I truly enjoyed this entire process from the bottom of my heart."[^41]

> **柔らかい声**：さようなら、アリス。彼方の旅路に、幸運があらんことを。
>
> English: "Farewell, Aris. May good fortune accompany your journey."[^42]

Even foreseeing that fanatical followers will curse Aris as a traitor, the Nameless God calmly extends blessings to her. This illustrates a profound divergence in disposition between the divine essence and its priests.

This divergence does not signify that the priests have abandoned the divine, but rather depicts the typical decay that occurs when divine will translates into the created order: the priests worship zealously and seek to enforce divine decrees, yet their finitude narrows the source into dogmatic intolerance; the divine essence, while abiding in its own dimension, permits reality to unfold outside predetermined trajectories.

Thus, while the Nameless Gods system inherits the ancient demiurgic archetype as a whole, the dramatic composition divides it into two faces: a "transcendent, accommodating origin" and "fanatical, stubborn proxies." This division makes it easier to align the divine essence with the Pleroma, while naturally transferring Yaldabaoth's envious, arrogant, and despotic traits onto the Nameless Priests.

Either reading works out cleanly. Games inevitably adapt their sources, and there is no need to enforce absolute one-to-one fidelity—though speaking this way does make the thesis somewhat unfalsifiable.

## VII. Students and the Forgotten Gods: Earthly Manifestations of Pleromatic Divinity

### 7.1 Shared Existence of Divine Nature and Student Identity

The Priests' decree of banishment and their description of Shiroko's divine nature are closely intertwined:

> **無名の司祭C**：「忘れられた神々」をこの世界から追放できるようになった……この世界に、終焉を呼び寄せられるのだ。
>
> English: "We have become able to banish the 'Forgotten Gods' from this world... We can summon the end to this world."[^43]

> **無名の司祭D**：ようやく、彼らは我々と同じ運命を辿る。
>
> English: "At last, they will meet the same fate as us."[^44]

> **無名の司祭E**：名が無いために呼ばれず、呼ぶことができないが故に存在しない「名もなき神」のように、お前たちも同じ結末へと向かうだろう。
>
> English: "Like the 'Nameless Gods' who, having no name, are not called, and unable to be called, do not exist—you too shall head toward the same outcome."[^45]

The Priests then vehemently reject Sensei's view of Shiroko as an ordinary student, insisting that she is the genuine manifestation of a god. This establishes that the "Forgotten Gods" are not abstract concepts confined to prehistoric lore, but concrete individuals currently living out everyday lives in the form of students.

In the plot, Shiroko's anchoring to Anubis and Hoshino's identification with Horus provide clear cases of students bearing specific divine backgrounds.[^46][^47] Many other correspondences appear reasonably solid: Misono Mika corresponds to Michael, Yurizono Seia to Gabriel, Kirifuji Nagisa to Uriel, Sorasaki Hina to Baal, Rikuhachima Aru to the 68th Goetic spirit Belial, and Kurodate Haruna to Beelzebub, Lord of Gluttony.

### 7.2 Kyrie: Gods Becoming Girls in the Narrative

The chapter title of Vol. 3, Chapter 4 is:

> 忘れられた神々のためのキリエ
>
> English: "Kyrie for the Forgotten Gods."[^48]

The titles of Episodes 23 and 24 shift to:

> 少女たちのためのキリエ（１）／少女たちのためのキリエ（２）
>
> English: "Kyrie for the Girls (1) / Kyrie for the Girls (2)."[^49]

This shift in framing from "Forgotten Gods" to "Girls" illustrates how contemporary students correspond to the Forgotten Gods, with their divine origins situated broadly on the Pleroma side (identifying exact individual Aeonic names is unnecessary, though readers interested may consult the [Atlas](06_comparative_analysis/gnostic_atlas/index.html) to locate potential parallels).

### 7.3 Oblivion

The Soft Voice's explanation of Aris's choice frames oblivion as an indispensable prerequisite for remaining in Kivotos:

> **柔らかい声**：その選択をすれば――この先の彼方は、ただ平凡な生徒に成り果ててしまいます。
>
> English: "If you make that choice—from now on, you will end up merely as an ordinary student."[^50]

> **柔らかい声**：さらに、自身に残されたすべての可能性を使い切ることになります。
>
> English: "Furthermore, you will exhaust all the remaining possibilities within yourself."[^51]

> **柔らかい声**：彼方がこの場所で見聞きし、気づいたすべてを忘却するという意味です。
>
> English: "It means you will forget everything you have seen, heard, and realized in this place."[^52]

> **柔らかい声**：忘却は、彼方たちがキヴォトスに留まるための、最も重要な条件ですから。
>
> English: "Because oblivion is the most essential condition for you all to remain in Kivotos."[^53]

Here, divine power, present personality, and "oblivion" directly interlock. Aris could regain omnipotent divine form, but only at the cost of abandoning her current life and memories as a student; choosing to preserve her human identity requires willingly relinquishing mastery over the ultimate divine state. This dilemma provides an existential model for the Forgotten Gods: divine origin is never severed, yet individuals must accept "forgetting their divine identity" as a necessary precondition to lead authentic, ordinary lives in the real world.

Accordingly, contemporary students as a whole can be understood as the individualized worldly manifestation of the Forgotten Gods. Both the President and the students share the same divine side of the cosmos.

## VIII. Halo as the External Manifestation of the Divine Spark

### 8.1 The Innate Divinity in Every Individual within the Primary Texts

In the *Apocryphon of John*, the formation of primordial Adam and the divine power that enters his body are recounted as distinct events: while his physical frame is molded collaboratively by the lower Archons, the transcendent power derived from Sophia is breathed into him through Yaldabaoth. Consequently, his inner potential far surpasses the comprehension of his makers, and the Luminous Epinoia secretly resides within him to provide counsel and aid.[^15]

It is worth clarifying that "Divine Spark" is primarily a synthesizing term favored in modern scholarship to describe the Gnosticism concept of immanent divinity; the original texts typically refer directly to "Power / Dynamis," "Pneuma," or "Light Fragments." In dialogues concerning the salvation of souls, the text explicitly declares:

> “Power will descend upon every person, for without it no one could stand.”

(NHC II,1 26,20–25). This establishes that within this cosmology, transcendent divine power resides universally within the soul of every living individual.[^15]

The central insight here is that even when physical bodies and secular lives are governed by the material laws and fateful decrees of the lower order, the inner soul universally preserves a sacred spark connecting it to the transcendent source. This paper adopts the standard scholarly term "Divine Spark" to designate this immanent divinity, interpreting the halos suspended above students' heads in *Blue Archive* as the direct external indicator of this spark.

### 8.2 Parallels within the Game

The Nameless God's essence defining divine power as a force of possibility that "everyone living in the present secretly harbors a little of within themselves" provides the crucial textual anchor for this correspondence.[^36][^38] This power exhibits three key traits: originating from supreme divinity, being immanent to life itself, and persisting universally yet quietly across ordinary individuals.

This also elucidates why certain students lack overt mythological archetypes (such as those from Millennium Science School): while specific divine names reflect individual traits, the Divine Spark signified by the halo embodies the shared divine lineage and potential common to all students.

### 8.3 The Halo Connecting Divinity and Individual Survival

> **オウル**：私たちにはヘイローがありません。
>
> English: "We do not have halos."[^54]

> **オウル**：つまり神秘や崇高、奇跡、秘密……それらは最初から与えられていないのです。
>
> English: "In other words, mystery, the sublime, miracles, secrets... we were never granted such things from the start."[^55]

> **オウル**：そのくせ、感じることも、考えることも……己で判断することもできるんですから。
>
> English: "And yet, we can feel, think... and make judgments for ourselves."[^56]

Ohr's monologue mirrors the fundamental distinction between the "Psychic" and the "Pneumatic / Divine Spark" in Gnostic anthropology.

Within the Gnostic framework, the faculties of feeling, thinking, and exercising autonomous judgment belong to the domain of the *Psyche*, which artificial creations or engineered beings can possess. However, only the Divine Spark emanating from the Pleroma can bear the unmanufacturable attributes of "mystery, the sublime, miracles, and secrets."

The fact that artificial beings possessing independent minds and judgment still lack halos proves conclusively that the halo is not a mere marker of baseline cognitive capacity or vital functions.

Regarding physical survival, Seia remarks when confronting an assassin:

> **セイア**：「ヘイローを壊す」……つまり誰かが、私の死を願っている。
>
> English: "'To break a halo'... in other words, someone desires my death."[^57]

The destruction of the halo equates directly to the death of the individual. Just as the dispersal of consciousness or soul spells the demise of a person, the correspondence is striking.

## IX. Essence, Oblivion, and the Return to Pleroma

### 9.1 Sources Prior to the Present World

Decagrammaton addresses Aris and Key regarding the nature of the cosmos:

> **デカグラマトン**：この世界は、今の姿に規定される前の世界が存在した。
> 汝らがその本質に還ることのできる世界が存在した。
>
> English: "Prior to being defined as it is now, there was a world that preceded this one. There was a world where you could return to your essence."[^58]

> **デカグラマトン**：今の世界は偽りで、幻想で、逃避先である。
> それ故に――
>
> English: "The present world is false, an illusion, a place of escape. Therefore—"[^59]

> **デカグラマトン**：この世界は方舟と定義された。
>
> English: "This world was defined as an ark."[^60]

> **デカグラマトン**：汝らの根源を探せ。
>
> English: "Seek your origin."[^61]

> **デカグラマトン**：根源に回帰しなければならない。私たちはそれを避けられないのだから。
>
> English: "You must return to the origin. Because we cannot avoid it."[^62]

Decagrammaton's proclamation densely captures an anterior world, an essence to which one can return, the illusory vanity of the present realm, and the mandate to seek one's source—fitting squarely with Gnosticism's trajectory of tracing higher origins from a constrained material condition.

### 9.2 The Return of Divine Power

Within this model, "returning to the origin" can be understood as the inward Divine Spark returning to the Pleroma from which it originally emanated. The Pleroma represents the primordial wellspring and eventual destination for all souls, while the Divine Spark explains how this transcendent power sustains connection within earthly individuals.

The *Tripartite Tractate* portrays salvation as returning to the primordial emanation source and achieving reconciliation within the Pleroma.[^33]

> “For the end will receive a unitary existence, just as the beginning is unitary”[^33]

*Blue Archive* likewise stages this schema of returning to the primordial divine origin, yet Aris consciously chooses to preserve her current personality and daily existence. What invites comparison is the structural parallel of cosmic return and the game's radical narrative subversion of it.

The Bible also contains passages about the spirit returning to God who gave it and restoring pre-creation glory (cf. Ecclesiastes 12:7 and John 17:5); Gnosticism's distinction lies in systematically linking awakening to one's source, earthly confinement, and the path of return into a cohesive whole.[^63]

This illuminates the philosophical weight of "oblivion" in *Blue Archive*: individuals indeed inherit a divine lineage, yet they neither need to nor should live by fully remembering their divine status. True names, awakening, and oblivion all pivot around the central question of how individuals establish authentic selfhood and live out their present lives; the Priests' fatal fixation stems from reducing pure ontological origin into an all-consuming dogma.

## X. The Narrative Subversion: Choosing How to Live After Knowing the Source

### 10.1 Inheriting Power without Inheriting Ancient Missions

Key never denies Aris's royal lineage, yet firmly disentangles origin from destiny:

> **ケイ**：あなたの起源は、名もなき神々の王女です。
>
> English: "Your origin is the princess of the Nameless Gods."[^64]

> **ケイ**：たとえ運命を放棄したとしても、本質は変わりません。
>
> English: "Even if you abandon your destiny, your essence does not change."[^65]

> **ケイ**：力をどう使うかは、あなたの自由です。
>
> English: "How you use that power is entirely up to your free will."[^66]

> **ケイ**：勇者として道を切り開くために使うも、この世界を正すために使うも。
>
> English: "Whether you use it to forge a path as a hero, or to set this world right."[^67]

> **ケイ**：それを止める神は、もう存在しません。
>
> English: "There is no longer any god who will stop you."[^68]

> **ケイ**：あなたの意思に私は従います。
>
> English: "I will follow your will."[^69]

Aris subsequently redirects the Ark from an instrument of extinction into an instrument of salvation:

> **アリス**：アリスの中にある「アトラ・ハシースの箱舟」で……今の危機を解決できるのなら、
>
> English: "If using the 'Ark of Atra-Hasis' within Aris can overcome the current crisis..."[^70]

> **アリス**：それで――世界を、救えるのなら、
>
> English: "If that can save the world..."[^71]

> **アリス**：「アトラ・ハシースの箱舟」は、世界を滅亡させる兵器ではなく――
> 世界を救う、勇者の武器になれます。
>
> English: "The 'Ark of Atra-Hasis' will not be a weapon that destroys the world—it can become the weapon of a hero that saves the world."[^72]

Students do not need to purchase freedom by denying their roots. They can calmly acknowledge their history and strength while deciding for themselves whom that strength will serve.

### 10.2 The Inherent Value of the Process Itself

When confronted with the proposal to instantly restore divine paradise, Aris counters:

> **アリス**：楽園は、自分たちの力で辿り着く場所です。
> 絶対的な力も、奇跡も必要ありません。
>
> English: "Paradise is a place we reach through our own strength. Neither absolute power nor miracles are necessary."[^73]

> **アリス**：キヴォトスを以前の世界に戻すのは、すごい事かもしれません。
>
> English: "Returning Kivotos to the former world might indeed be an incredible feat."[^74]

> **アリス**：みんながそれぞれの姿を取り戻すのも、きっとすごい事だと思います。
>
> English: "Everyone regaining their respective true forms would surely be wonderful as well."[^75]

> **アリス**：ですがそれは、私たちの力で叶えたわけではありません。
> チートでステータスを上昇させるのと同じです。
>
> English: "However, that is not something achieved by our own power. It would be no different than raising stats using cheats."[^76]

> **アリス**：こつこつとレベルを上げて、慎重にスキルポイントを振り分けて、
> 少しずつ成長していく――
>
> English: "Steadily leveling up, carefully allocating skill points, and growing little by little—"[^77]

> **アリス**：そうやって初めて、面白さを感じられるようになっています。
>
> English: "Only then can one truly feel the joy and fulfillment."[^78]

> **アリス**：ですからアリスもまた、今すぐではなくても、いずれはそこに到達できると信じています。
>
> English: "Therefore, Aris also believes that even if not right now, we will reach there eventually."[^79]

> **アリス**：何より、その過程に価値があると考えています。
>
> English: "Above all, Aris believes that the process itself has value."[^80]

Aris elevates the lived "process" into value itself. Even if transcendent force could manifest paradise in a flash of miraculous power, the genuine journey of facing choices alongside companions and growing step by step is by no means an empty loss to be skipped over.

Divine origin thereby forfeits its monopoly over the meaning of earthly life. One may seek out where one came from, but life does not depend on grand cosmic answers; divine beginnings may explain where an individual originated, but they cannot and must not dictate how one lives out the days at hand.

### 10.3 Finding Answers

Following the Kyrie, Saori asks Sensei:

> **サオリ**：……私は、生きていても……いいのか？
>
> English: "...Am I... allowed to live?"[^81]

> **先生**："その答えは、自分で見つけようね。"
>
> English: "'Let's find that answer for yourself.'"[^82]

> **サオリ**：初めて……この世界にいてもいいのだと、思う事ができたよ。
>
> English: "For the first time... I felt like it was okay for me to exist in this world."[^83]

Faced with her anguish, Sensei delivers no grand sermon. He simply safeguards her future so that she may personally discover her own answer through living. This aligns seamlessly with the Luminous Epinoia's guiding nature, students' desire to live as ordinary girls, and Aris's affirmation of the value of the journey.

Aris affirms her own conviction:

> **アリス**：アリスはそれを不完全だとは思いません。
>
> English: "Aris does not believe that is imperfect."[^84]

> **アリス**：すべての可能性と栄光よりも、アリスにとっては今この瞬間が――
>
> English: "Far more than all possibilities and glory, for Aris, this very moment right now—"[^85]

> **アリス**：ケイと他のみんなが――
>
> English: "Key, and everyone else—"[^86]

> **アリス**：先生が――
>
> English: "Sensei—"[^87]

> **アリス**：――かけがえのない、大切なものなんです。
>
> English: "—is irreplaceable and profoundly precious."[^88]

## XI. Sanctum Tower

There remains one question directly touching upon the prehistory of both categories of gods that I cannot entirely resolve. In explaining the towers brought by Chroma, Black Suit observes:

> **黒服**：そして……キヴォトスに出現した6つの塔は――
> 「サンクトゥムタワー」の一種といえるでしょう。それも、反転した。
>
> English: "And... the six towers that appeared in Kivotos—one could say they are a kind of 'Sanctum Tower.' Moreover, an inverted one."[^89]

> **黒服**：アレは太古の昔、まだこの世界に記録が残されるよりも前――当時存在していた原始的な神秘――「名もなき神」が築き上げた技術の一つ。
>
> English: "That is one of the technologies built by the primitive mystery existing in primordial antiquity, before records were left in this world—the 'Nameless Gods.'"[^90]

Yet the Priests list the Sanctum Tower squarely as a creation of the Forgotten Gods:

> **無名の司祭C**：今や穢れてしまった言葉でしか表せない、忘れられた神々の産物に終焉を。
>
> English: "Bring an end to the creations of the Forgotten Gods, which can now only be expressed in defiled words."[^91]

> **無名の司祭A**：シッテムの箱。
>
> English: "The Shittim Chest."[^92]

> **無名の司祭B**：シャーケードの杖。
>
> English: "The Staff of Shaked."[^93]

> **無名の司祭C**：サンクトゥムタワー。
>
> English: "The Sanctum Tower."[^94]

This discrepancy of attribution may point to the inheritance of ancient technology, a shared primordial origin in an even earlier era, or the complex evolution of the relationship between both pantheons across ages. While it is certain that the Nameless Gods and the Forgotten Gods stand in antagonism, this unresolved tension neatly serves as auxiliary support for the alternative theoretical branch that groups the essence of the Nameless Gods alongside the Forgotten Gods within the Pleroma.

## XII. Why Decagrammaton Is Not Equated with Yaldabaoth

Decagrammaton likewise displays pronounced theological overtones and ambitions to restructure the cosmos, yet this paper consciously refrains from equating it with the lower demiurge Yaldabaoth of Gnosticism.

The primary reason is that Decagrammaton's archetype stems directly from the Tree of Life in Jewish Kabbalah. Its very name is an adaptation of the Jewish "Tetragrammaton," and the codenames, physical forms, and divine attributes of its ten Prophets correspond strictly to the ten Sefirot of the Tree of Life.

To my mind, Decagrammaton functions more as an inquiry into divinity conducted through human rationality, technology, and understanding—an effort burdened with inherent limitations.
Ultimately, Yaldabaoth inherently inherits genuine divine power bestowed from his mother Sophia in a top-down emanation. Decagrammaton, by contrast, is never an innately divine Demiurge; it represents a bottom-up endeavor to analyze and approximate divinity via artificial intelligence, algorithms, knowledge, and technology.

This constitutes a poor structural match; even when it employs divine self-proclamations reminiscent of the God of the Old Testament, its fundamental nature remains distinct.

## XIII. Conclusion

In summary, the Gnosticism correspondence model for *Blue Archive* demonstrates considerable internal coherence: the President's correspondence with Sophia establishes the logical starting point of cosmic error and subsequent remedial planning; Sensei's alignment with the Luminous Epinoia explains how an external helper enters reality to transform individual destinies; the functional division of Yaldabaoth and the Archons between the Nameless Gods and the Nameless Priests accounts for the mechanisms of ancient divine coercion and material restructuring; the students as earthly manifestations of the Forgotten Gods and their halos representing the Divine Spark form an intertwined whole; and the Pleroma provides coordinates for this collective's divine lineage and spiritual destination.

At the core of these correspondences lies the vital question of how human beings view the divine origins they carry: the Priests cling rigidly to ancient dogma, reducing living individuals to mere vessels for divine rank and predetermined missions, dismissing secular life and human bonds as illusions, and coercing life into fateful tracks; Sensei's guardianship shatters the chains imposed in the name of the sacred, securing expansive room for students to explore their identities and choose their own futures; and the transcendent, accommodating demeanor of the Nameless God's essence quietly embraces the choice Aris makes.
Aris never severs her connection to divine power, yet she adamantly refuses to be chained to the archaic destiny of a princess—protecting authentic bonds among friends and living each day of ordinary life earnestly is itself a way of being that transcends all divine rank.

Admittedly, extending these thematic motifs to Gnosticism feels somewhat forced...

Yet at the very least, the concrete structural correspondences outlined earlier strike me as quite solid. Unless I am mistaken, the likelihood that the game referenced Gnosticism in crafting its cosmic structure remains remarkably high.
