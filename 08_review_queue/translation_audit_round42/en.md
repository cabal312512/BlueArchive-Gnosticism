## 3. 中文第7行对应段落

恢复可能、更像、往往等限定；删除彻底颠覆、客观意义、凡人等增译；保留绝不应、永远不会及原文对日常价值的明确判断。

**中文依据**

在许多宗教与神话观念中，人当下的现实生活很少被赋予自足的价值。在这类宇宙论看来，物质受造界往往带有先天的不完美，而肉身的存在更像是一种暂时的困顿或遗忘；人在世俗中经历的喜怒哀乐与琐碎日常，统统被视作需要被超越的过渡。
在这种逻辑下，生命的终极意义被寄托在“回归”之中——只有唤醒内在的灵性、摆脱现实的束缚，重新回归最初那个崇高的神圣本源，个体才被认为完成了救赎，生命才算真正获得了圆满与解脱。
游戏可能通过这套模型给出的回答颠覆了这一点：神圣的来源或许解释了灵魂“从何而来”，但绝不应规定生命“为何而活”。即便人们携带着神性的本源，生命的展开永远不会以解答宏大课题为前提。人们在正视当下的生活前，不必先完成对自身来源的追问、去承担某种预定的使命、找到生命的意义等等。
比起那些高维的永远无法被我们理解且无需被理解的东西，在日复一日的“平凡”中所度过的“日常”——那些与同伴共同欢笑、犯错、成长的“过程本身”，才是我们真正该珍视的。既然已经到了这个世界上，就不该本末倒置，去研究来之前或走之后的东西。认真过完这一生才是最重要的。
且所谓的“奇迹”，也往往并不来自某种超越常理的力量，而恰恰诞生于人们在平凡而脆弱的日常中彼此守护、自由选择所散发出的温度。拒绝让神圣的来源反客为主，正是为了守住这份真真切切、闪闪发光的日常奇迹。

**修改前**

```markdown
In many religious and mythological traditions, human existence in the here-and-now is rarely accorded self-sufficient value. Within such cosmologies, the material created realm is frequently regarded as inherently deficient, while bodily existence is seen as merely a temporary state of entrapment or oblivion; the joys, sorrows, and trivialities of everyday secular life are universally subordinated as a transitional phase that must be transcended.
Under this logic, the ultimate meaning of life is anchored entirely in "return"—only by awakening one's innate spiritual nature, casting off the shackles of the material world, and returning to the transcendent primordial source can the individual attain salvation and fulfill life's true completeness and liberation.
Yet the answer *Blue Archive* offers through this archetype thoroughly subverts this premise: while divine origins may explain "where the soul came from," they must never dictate "what one lives for." Even if individuals carry a divine origin within them, living one's life is never contingent upon first solving grandiose existential puzzles. Before embracing the life right before them, people do not need to interrogate their ontological origins, shoulder preordained cosmic missions, or discover an abstract "meaning of life."
Compared to higher dimensions that we can never comprehend—and have no need to comprehend—the "daily life" lived in day-to-day "ordinariness"—the "process itself" of laughing, making mistakes, and growing alongside companions—is what truly warrants our cherishing. Having arrived in this world, one should not invert priorities by obsessing over what came before birth or what lies after departure. Living this present life earnestly is what matters above all.
Furthermore, so-called "miracles" rarely stem from supernatural powers that violate normal reality; rather, they are born from the warmth radiated when ordinary people guard one another and make free choices within their fragile, everyday lives. Refusing to let divine essence usurp human existence is precisely what preserves this authentic, shining daily miracle.
```

**修改后**

```markdown
In many religious and mythological conceptions, people’s present, real lives are seldom granted value in their own right. In such cosmologies, the material creation often carries an inherent imperfection, while bodily existence seems more like a temporary state of confinement or forgetting; the joys, sorrows, and everyday details of secular life are all treated as a transition to be transcended.
Within this logic, life’s ultimate meaning is placed in a “return”: only by awakening one’s inner spirituality, escaping the constraints of reality, and returning to the original, exalted divine source is the individual considered to have attained salvation, and life to have reached fulfillment and liberation.
The answer the game may offer through this model overturns that view: a divine origin may explain “where the soul comes from,” but it must never determine “what life is for.” Even if people carry a divine origin within them, living never depends on answering grand questions first. Before facing the life before them, people do not need to finish investigating their origins, take on some predetermined mission, find life’s meaning, and so on.
Rather than those higher-dimensional things that we can never understand and have no need to understand, what we should truly cherish is the “everyday life” spent in day after day of the “ordinary”—the “process itself” of laughing, making mistakes, and growing alongside our companions. Since we are already in this world, we should not reverse our priorities by studying what came before our arrival or what follows our departure. Living this life with care is what matters most.
And so-called “miracles” often do not come from some power beyond ordinary understanding, but from the warmth of people protecting one another and choosing freely within their ordinary, fragile daily lives. Refusing to let divine origins take precedence over life is precisely how we protect these real, shining everyday miracles.
```
