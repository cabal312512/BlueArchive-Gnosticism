# Notes on Terminology in the Text

[Return to the paper](gnostic_correspondence_en.md) · [View the relationship diagram](gnostic_atlas.html)

This guide first explains the relationships in Apocryphon of John, the paper’s main source, then adds other traditions and terminology. The basic sequence is: the highest source manifests the divine world; Sophia belongs to this world, yet when creating alone she gives birth to Yaldabaoth; Yaldabaoth and the Archons organize the lower world and shape the human body, while within the human there is also a power from a higher world, and the Luminous Epinoia helps humans recognize this.

## The Divine World and Main Characters

**Monad / Invisible Spirit / Father**: The Apocryphon of John uses these designations to speak about the highest source. Monad emphasizes 'one'; Invisible Spirit emphasizes the invisible Spirit; Father emphasizes that all things come from it. It is self-sufficient, unbegotten, and has no being higher than itself; Barbelo and the subsequent divine beings unfold from it. It stands at the source of the entire generative relation, while Yaldabaoth is a creator who appears later and shapes the lower world.

**Pleroma**: means 'fullness' or 'completion,' and refers to the complete realm jointly constituted by divine beings. It can be understood as the totality of the upper world, and Sophia and other Aeons are beings within it. Within this totality there are still different relations and levels; saying that certain beings all belong to the Pleroma side does not require forcing them one by one into a roster with a fixed number of names. The paper uses it to summarize the higher world with which the students’ divinity is associated.

**Aeon / Aeons**: singular and plural. It often refers to eternal beings unfolding from the divine source, can also refer to the corresponding realms, and some texts also use it to denote eras or cosmic regions. In the Apocryphon of John, each of the Four Luminaries has three attendant Aeons, twelve in total; another Valentinian list has 8 + 10 + 12, thirty in total. Twelve is the number of a specific grouping and cannot be taken as a unified total for Gnosticism as a whole.

**Barbelo**: the First Thought of the highest source, and also its image and the womb of all things, called Mother–Father. She can first be understood as the first divine manifestation of the highest source. She requests divine attributes from the root and participates in the manifestation of Autogenes / Christ; the subsequent divine world unfolds from this. Sophia is another being within this unfolding; the two cannot be merged merely because both are called 'divine mother.'

**Autogenes / Christ**: Autogenes means 'self-begotten one.' The Apocryphon of John narrates him as the only-begotten light: the Invisible Spirit and Barbelo jointly participate in his manifestation, he is anointed, and he organizes the subsequent divine realms. The Christ here is a figure in the upper-world narrative and occupies a different position from Yaldabaoth, the maker of the ordinary human body.

**Four Luminaries**: the four luminous beings Harmozel, Oroiael, Daveithe, and Eleleth, also associated with the organization and dwelling places of the divine realm. Adamas, Seth, their descendants, and different groups are respectively placed in the relevant realms. Their respective three attendants make up the twelve Aeons mentioned above; according to the interpretation adopted in this paper, Sophia is the Wisdom in the list of Eleleth's attendants.

**Sophia**: the name means 'wisdom'; she is an Aeon in the divine world. The Apocryphon of John says that, without obtaining the Spirit's consent and without acting together with her consort, she attempted to create alone, and as a result gave birth to Yaldabaoth, who was incongruous with the divine world. She hid her offspring outside, later recognized the defect, repented, and sought a remedy. The power Yaldabaoth possesses comes from her; the power later entering Adam is also an important link in the process of remedy. The paper compares her fault, repentance, and remedy with the General Student Council President.

**The Ninth**: the position where Sophia temporarily resides after her imbalance, above her offspring, until the defect is corrected. It explains where she is placed at this stage of the story; her original identity as belonging to the upper world still needs to be understood together with this. The paper uses this to compare the state in which the president, after admitting a mistake, withdraws behind the scenes.

**Demiurge**: originally means 'craftsman'; in these discussions it refers to the creator who shapes and organizes the world, and it is a role designation. In the Apocryphon of John, Yaldabaoth takes on this role; Valentinian materials such as the Letter to Flora, by contrast, describe the Demiurge as a just being lower than the highest perfect being but higher than the evil opposite. Therefore this word first explains 'what one does'; its character and status must then be examined in the specific text.

**Yaldabaoth**: the offspring Sophia created alone, and the Chief Archon and Demiurge in the Apocryphon of John. The text also calls him by names such as Saklas and Samael. He possesses his mother's power but does not know its higher source, and considers himself the only god; he then produces ruling powers and, imitating the upper world, organizes the lower world. He and the Archons make Adam's body, but under the helpers’ guidance he blows the mother's power into Adam, so that human cognition instead surpasses that of the creator. The paper maps this structure of creation and domination onto the side of the Nameless Gods.

**Archon / Archons**: means 'ruler,' singular and plural. Yaldabaoth is the chief, and the lower rulers and powers he produces participate in organizing the world, making the human body, and maintaining control through such means as forgetfulness, desire, and fate. The Apocryphon of John simultaneously preserves descriptions such as the twelve Authorities, the arrangement of the seven heavens, and the sevenfold powers; these lists are related, and all the names that have appeared cannot be directly pieced together into the same list of seven. The paper mainly uses the structure of the seven heavens and the lower rulers.

**Adam**: the ancestor of lower humanity, to be distinguished from Adamas, the upper-world prototype of the human. The Archons first shape his body; only after the power from Sophia is blown into it does he obtain life and higher cognition. The Luminous Epinoia secretly helps him; the rulers impose restrictions because his cognition surpasses their own. This story narrates the making of the human body and the origin of the inner power separately, and is also important background for the paper's discussion of the Divine Spark.

**Luminous Epinoia / Epinoia**: Epinoia carries the sense of insight and reflection; Luminous Epinoia can be understood as 'luminous insight.' In the Apocryphon of John, she is a helper sent from the upper world, called Life, hidden in Adam, helping him recognize his own origin, descendants, and the way of return, and participating in correcting the mother's defect. She acts both as a being in the myth and has the function of awakening cognition within the human. The paper compares this role of assistance and guidance with Sensei.

**Pronoia**: has meanings such as foreknowledge, care, and Providence, and in the Apocryphon of John is closely connected with Barbelo's identity as First Thought. At the end of the long recension, Pronoia is the narrator, recounting her actions of descending, entering the darkness, awakening the bound, and applying the Five Seals. Both Epinoia and Pronoia participate in assistance, but when citing specific actions one must still clearly identify who is speaking and who is acting.

[The above relationships are mainly found in the Apocryphon of John](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/apocryphonjohn.html). More groupings and differences between versions can be viewed in the [atlas](gnostic_atlas.html).

## Inner Nature and Salvation

**Divine Spark**: a term the paper uses to summarize the 'divine spark,' the divine source or component within the individual. In the relevant story of the Apocryphon of John, the specific threads are the power from Sophia entering Adam and the upper-world assistance working within the human. This summary shows that although humans live in the lower world, they are still connected to a higher source; it does not require that every person was originally a particular Aeon with a proper name. On this basis, the paper understands the students’ halos as an external representation of divinity.

**Power / Dynamis**: power, capacity, and in myth can also refer to a being with a corresponding function. Not every occurrence of power refers to the same thing. The paper mainly focuses on the power that Sophia possesses, that Yaldabaoth obtains from her, and that later enters Adam; the key here is how it is transmitted, not judging identity solely by the word 'power.'

**Pneuma**: usually translated as 'spirit.' In these discussions, it often refers to the component related to the divine source, life, and spirituality; pneumatic is the corresponding adjective. It emphasizes what nature or constitution a person has.

**Psyche**: usually translated as 'soul,' involving life and mind. Some systems place it between Pneuma and matter: it is not simply the body, and is distinct from the higher spiritual component. Specific usage still varies from text to text.

**Psychic / Pneumatic**: respectively mean 'belonging to the soul' and 'belonging to the spirit.' Some Valentinian materials use them together with material or hylic to discuss three constitutions or human types, and different responses to the savior. Pneumatic describes a property or type; Divine Spark, by contrast, is this paper's summary of the inner divine component, and the two are not the same kind of classification unit.

**Light Fragments**: fragments of light, an image term the paper uses to describe divine light as dispersed and remaining within individuals. The point of understanding is that dispersed individuals remain related to the higher light; one should not conclude on this basis that every source text describes the same process of fragmentation.

**Heimarmene**: fate; in the Apocryphon of John it is a bondage arranged by the lower ruling powers, involving dominion over time, temporal order, and the created world, and connected with forgetfulness. It causes human life to be regulated by an external order. The paper uses this to compare the mission and use that the Nameless Priests prescribe for the individual. [Related text](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/apocryphonjohn.html)

## Names Used in Other Versions

**Valentinian**: the tradition formed by Valentinus and his followers. Related texts also discuss the highest source, the Pleroma, the defect, and salvation, but the arrangement of figures need not follow the Apocryphon of John: for example, the Tripartite Tractate places the core story of deviation and restoration on the Logos. The paper's supplement concerning Sophia being 'the youngest' comes from this tradition's account.

**Duodecad**: a group made up of twelve. One Valentinian list recorded by Irenaeus first has the Ogdoad of eight, then the Decad of ten and the Duodecad of twelve, totaling thirty. Sophia is at the end of the last group, paired with Theletos. [Against Heresies I.1](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103101.htm), [I.2](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103102.htm)

**Achamoth / Horos**: In the above Valentinian account, Horos performs the functions of boundary, maintenance, and separation, restoring Sophia; the Enthymesis and passion separated out from her constitute the external Achamoth. Achamoth receives help outside the Pleroma and is related to the Demiurge and the production of the divine seed. This account distinguishes the Sophia within the Pleroma from the external Achamoth. [Against Heresies I.2–5](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103102.htm)

## Other Religious Backgrounds Appearing in the Text

**Kabbalah**: a Jewish mystical tradition that discusses how the infinite God manifests, how the world unfolds, and the relationship between humans and God. The paper mentions it in order to explain that some religious symbols in the game also have other intellectual backgrounds.

**Sefirah / Sefirot**: singular and plural, referring to the ten divine manifestations or emanations in Kabbalah, such as Wisdom, Understanding, and so on. They form an interrelated structure that explains how the infinite God and the world come into contact.

**Tree of Life**: a common diagram that draws the Sefirot and their relations, usually with ten nodes and connecting paths. The positions, groupings, and interconnections of the nodes together express meaning; it cannot be seen merely as a roster of ten figures. [Explanation of Sefirot](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/sefirot/)

**Tetragrammaton**: the four-letter divine name, usually transliterated as YHWH, the name of God in ancient Israel and Jewish tradition. Its lexical meaning and the background of its religious use, and how later different traditions interpret this God, are two questions that need to be understood in connection. [Note on the name](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/6359796/jewish/The-Tetragrammaton.htm)

**Kyrie**: the Greek form of calling 'Lord'; Kyrie eleison means 'Lord, have mercy,' used in supplicatory liturgy. The game's chapter title borrows this expression, and the paper focuses on the change in what the title refers to, from gods to girls.

## Word Usage

**Gnosticism**: a general term for a group of ancient religious ideas and traditions. This paper focuses on their discussions of a higher source, the lower created order, the divine component within humans, and knowledge and salvation. This name helps to categorize the related traditions; specific myths are still based on each individual document.

**Gnosis**: knowledge or cognition; in the relevant religious context, spiritual knowledge, referring to knowledge concerning one's own situation, divine origin, and salvation. It is a kind of knowledge, not the name of an entire religious tradition.

**Gnostic**: usually an adjective meaning 'related to the relevant traditions,' and can also be a noun referring to an adherent. Gnostic texts are the relevant documents. When the paper discusses the entire intellectual tradition, it uses Gnosticism. [Terminology background](https://iep.utm.edu/gnostic/)

## Documents and Abbreviations

**Apocryphon of John**: framed as the savior revealing secret teachings to John, it narrates in sequence the highest source and upper world, Sophia's fault, Yaldabaoth and his rulers, and the formation and salvation of the human. Four main manuscript witnesses survive, divided into a long recension and a short recension; they share a skeleton and also preserve variant readings. The paper mainly uses this text, often abbreviated AJ. [English translation](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/apocryphonjohn.html)

**Tripartite Tractate**: a long text of the Valentinian tradition, discussing the Father, the Son, and the divine totality, the deviation of the Logos, the formation of the world and the human, and salvation and restoration. The final unity cited in the paper belongs to its positive account of salvation. [English translation](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/tripartite.html)

**Gospel of Truth**: revolves around the Father, ignorance, forgetfulness, and knowledge, telling how the savior enables people to know their origin, escape deficiency, and obtain rest. The opening of the paper quotes its expression about returning to the place that gave it its essence. [English translation](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/gospeltruth.html)

**Letter to Flora**: a letter from Ptolemy to Flora; by discussing the law and its framer, it distinguishes the highest perfect being, the just but imperfect creator, and the evil opposite. The paper uses it to show that the Demiurge can have a character and position different from Yaldabaoth's common stereotype. [English translation](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/flora.html)

**Against Heresies**: Irenaeus's work criticizing the teachings he opposed, while also preserving his retellings of different systems. When citing it, one must both see which set of views he records and know that this is an external report by a critic. The paper uses its material on Sophia being the youngest, her imbalance, and the remedy. [Cited chapters](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103102.htm)

**Nag Hammadi Library**: the general name for a group of ancient manuscripts discovered in Egypt in 1945, preserving many related documents. It is a collection of documents, not the unified scripture of a single tradition. The Nag Hammadi Library in English is the modern English translation collection used by the paper.

**NHC / BG**: abbreviations for manuscript locations. NHC is Nag Hammadi Codex; NHC II,1 means the first tractate in Codex II. BG is Berlin Codex; BG 8502,2 means the second tractate in that manuscript. The page and line numbers that follow locate passages in the manuscript, not the page numbers of an arbitrarily downloaded PDF.

**The Apocryphon of John: Synopsis of Nag Hammadi Codices II,1; III,1; and IV,1 with BG 8502,2**: a four-manuscript synoptic edition edited by Michael Waldstein and Frederik Wisse, placing the corresponding texts side by side for easy comparison of the long and short recensions and the differences among the manuscripts. Synopsis in the paper is the abbreviation for this book.

**Bible / Old Testament**: Bible refers to the Christian scriptures as a whole, and Old Testament is the part that inherits the tradition of the ancient Israelite scriptures. Genesis discusses creation and early humanity; Ecclesiastes discusses life, labor, and the end; John discusses Jesus's life and teachings; Hebrews discusses Jesus, faith, and promise, and the 'abiding city' quoted at the beginning of the paper comes from 13:14; Revelation depicts through visions the last things, such as judgment and a new heaven and a new earth.

**Dao De Jing / Amitābha Sūtra / Manichaean Hymnscroll**: The opening of the paper also cites three other types of material. The Dao De Jing uses returning to the root and returning to destiny to discuss the changes of all things; the Amitābha Sūtra describes Sukhāvatī and urges people to make vows to be reborn there; the Manichaean Hymnscroll refers to the Manichaean 'Lower Hymnscroll' used here, praying for the dead that the luminous nature may return to the world of light. They are used to illustrate the questions of origin, liberation, and life in the present world discussed in the paper; for the context of each passage, see the [verification record](09_religious_comparison/opening_context_review_round48.md).
