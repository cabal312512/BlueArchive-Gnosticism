# Glossary of Terms Used in the Paper

[Back to Paper](gnostic_correspondence_en.md)

## Three Easily Confused Terms

**Gnosticism**: An umbrella term for a group of ancient religious ideas and traditions. The ideas most relevant to this paper are that there is a higher divine source above the creator of the real world, that people retain a divine element from that source within themselves, and that recognizing their origins is connected with escaping bondage and attaining salvation.

**Gnosis**: Literally “recognition” or “knowledge”; here it is usually rendered as “spiritual knowledge.”

**Gnostic**: Usually an adjective meaning “related to the traditions described above”; it can also be a noun for an adherent. For example, Gnostic texts means texts related to these traditions. The paper consistently uses Gnosticism when discussing the whole tradition of thought. [Terminological background](https://iep.utm.edu/gnostic/)

## The Divine Realm and Major Figures

**Father**: Means “father.” In texts discussing the highest source, it is a name for the supreme divine being.

**Pleroma**: Literally “fullness” or “plenitude.” For now, it can be understood as the whole formed by divine beings and their complete life. The paper uses it to explain the higher divine source and the direction of return.

**Aeon / Aeons**: Singular and plural. In the relevant myths, they often refer to eternal beings unfolding from a divine source, and can also concern the realms they inhabit. Their numbers and relationships vary between texts.

**Sophia**: The name means “wisdom.” Apocryphon of John tells how she acts alone, gives birth to deficient offspring, then repents and participates in putting things right. The paper uses this to compare her with the General Student Council President.

**The Ninth**: The place where Sophia temporarily resides after repenting in Apocryphon of John, above her offspring. The paper uses this name when discussing her withdrawal behind the scenes.

**Demiurge**: Originally “craftsman”; here it means the creator who shapes the real world, a name for a role.

**Yaldabaoth**: The Demiurge in Apocryphon of John, born of Sophia. He has power from his mother but does not know the higher source, and regards himself as the only god.

**Archon / Archons**: Singular and plural, meaning “ruler” and “rulers.” The paper mainly refers to the lower ruling powers established by Yaldabaoth. They help shape the world, make human bodies, and impose bonds.

**Adam**: The first ancestor in the myth. Apocryphon of John describes the shaping of his body and the entry of divine power into it separately.

**Luminous Epinoia / Epinoia**: The former can be understood as “luminous insight.” In Apocryphon of John, she helps Adam, dwelling secretly within him and helping him recognize his source and the path of return. [These figures and relationships in Apocryphon of John](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/apocryphonjohn.html)

## The Human Interior and Salvation

**Divine Spark**: The “divine spark” used in the paper to summarize the divine element within people. It helps explain how individuals remain connected to a higher source even while living in the real world.

**Power / Dynamis**: Meaning "power" or "capacity." Its precise referent depends on context; the paper focuses on the power derived from Sophia that entered Adam.

**Pneuma**: Usually translated as “spirit”; in the relevant discussions it often refers to human spirituality or a divine origin.

**Psyche**: Usually translated as “soul”; it also concerns life and mental activity. It is distinguished from Pneuma in some systems.

**Psychic / Pneumatic**: Adjectives related to Psyche and Pneuma respectively, often rendered as “of the soul / of the spirit.” In some Valentinian materials they are also used to classify types of people. Pneumatic describes a property or type; Divine Spark is a collective name for the inner divine component. [The human interior and related distinctions](https://iep.utm.edu/gnostic/)

**Light Fragments**: Literally "fragments of light," used in the paper to describe the image of divine luminescence dispersed and preserved within individuals.

**Heimarmene**: Means “fate.” In Apocryphon of John, lower rulers use it to bind gods, humans, and other beings. The paper uses it to compare the missions and arrangements imposed by the Priests. [Relevant passage](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/apocryphonjohn.html)

**Valentinian**: The tradition of thought formed by Valentinus and his followers. The paper’s additional point about Sophia being “the youngest” comes from this tradition’s materials.

**Duodecad**: A group of twelve. In the system described by Irenaeus, Sophia is placed last in this group; twelve here is the number of members in a group. [Against Heresies I.2](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103102.htm)

## Other Religious Backgrounds Appearing in the Text

**Kabbalah**: The Jewish mystical tradition, addressing how the divine manifests and how the world unfolds.

**Sefirah / Sefirot**: Singular and plural. The ten divine manifestations or emanations in Kabbalah, used to explain the connection between God and the world.

**Tree of Life**: A common diagram organizing the Sefirot and their relationships, usually drawn as ten nodes and connecting paths. [Explanation of Sefirot](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/sefirot/)

**Tetragrammaton**: Meaning "four letters," designating the divine name transliterated as YHWH. [Explanation of Name](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/6359796/jewish/The-Tetragrammaton.htm)

**Kyrie**: The Greek form used to address “Lord,” often found in the liturgical plea for mercy, Kyrie eleison.

## Literature and Abbreviations

**Apocryphon of John**: A text framed around the resurrected Savior revealing secret teachings to John. It describes the divine world, Sophia, Yaldabaoth, human formation, and salvation. It is the principal ancient text used in the paper, sometimes abbreviated as **AJ** in the notes. [English translation](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/apocryphonjohn.html)

**Tripartite Tractate**: A long text in the Valentinian tradition discussing divine origins, the formation of the world and humanity, and salvation and restoration. The paper uses passages on return and reconciliation. [English translation](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/tripartite.html)

**Letter to Flora**: A letter from Ptolemy to Flora discussing the law and its maker. It understands the world’s creator as a just being between the supreme perfect being and the evil adversary. The paper uses it to explain differences in depictions of the Demiurge. [English translation](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/flora.html)

**Against Heresies**: Irenaeus’s work criticizing doctrines he opposed; it also preserves his accounts of the related systems. The paper cites its passages on Valentinian Sophia. [Cited section](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103102.htm)

**Nag Hammadi Library**: The collective name for ancient manuscripts discovered in Egypt in 1945, preserving many related texts. **The Nag Hammadi Library in English** is the modern collection of English translations used in the paper.

**NHC / BG**: Abbreviations for manuscript references. NHC stands for Nag Hammadi Codex; for example, NHC II,1 means the first text in Codex II. BG stands for Berlin Codex, and BG 8502,2 means the second text in that codex. The page and line numbers that follow allow precise lookup.

**The Apocryphon of John: Synopsis of Nag Hammadi Codices II,1; III,1; and IV,1 with BG 8502,2**: A four-manuscript synopsis edited by Michael Waldstein and Frederik Wisse. **Synopsis** in the paper’s notes is its shortened title.

**Bible / Old Testament**: The former is the collective name for Christian scripture; the latter is the part that carries on the ancient Israelite scriptural tradition. **Genesis** tells stories of creation and early humanity; **Ecclesiastes** discusses life, labor, and the end; **John** is a Gospel about Jesus’s life and teachings; **Revelation** depicts scenes of the end through visions of judgment, a new heaven and earth, and so on.
