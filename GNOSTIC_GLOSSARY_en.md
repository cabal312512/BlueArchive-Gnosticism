# Glossary: Gnosticism Terms and Literature

[Back to Paper](gnostic_correspondence_en.md)


## Three Easily Confused Terms

**Gnosticism**: An umbrella term for a cluster of ancient religious traditions and philosophical movements. The core concept most relevant to this paper is that above the creator of the material world exists a higher transcendent divine source, and human beings internally retain divine components originating from that realm; awakening to one's true origin is intrinsically linked to liberation from earthly confinement and attaining salvation.

**Gnosis**: Literally meaning "recognition" or "knowledge," typically translated in spiritual contexts as experiential or intuitive spiritual knowledge.

**Gnostic**: Primarily an adjective meaning "pertaining to the aforementioned traditions," and secondarily a noun designating adherents. For example, "Gnostic texts" refers to literature from these traditions. This paper consistently uses Gnosticism when referring to the philosophical tradition as a whole. [Context on Terminology](https://iep.utm.edu/gnostic/)

## The Divine Realm and Major Figures

**Father**: Signifying the primordial source. In relevant literature discussing supreme ontology, it serves as the designation for the supreme transcendent divine principle.

**Pleroma**: Literally "fullness" or "plenitude." Understood as the divine totality composed of transcendent divine entities and their complete life. The paper employs this term to designate the higher divine source and the ultimate direction of return.

**Aeon / Aeons**: Singular and plural. In relevant mythologies, these designate eternal divine entities emanating from the primordial source, as well as the ontological realms they inhabit. Their numbers and relations vary across texts.

**Sophia**: Literally "Wisdom." In the Apocryphon of John, she acts autonomously without her counterpart, giving rise to a deficient offspring, subsequently repents, and actively participates in cosmic remediation. The paper uses this archetype to parallel the General Student Council President.

**The Ninth**: In the Apocryphon of John, the intermediate sphere above her offspring where Sophia temporarily resides following her repentance, awaiting cosmic rectification. The paper references this when discussing her retreat behind the scenes.

**Demiurge**: Originally meaning "craftsman" or "artisan," here designating the creator who forms the material world—a functional role.

**Yaldabaoth**: The Demiurge in the Apocryphon of John, offspring of Sophia. Inheriting power from his mother while remaining ignorant of higher sources, he falsely proclaims himself the sole god.

**Archon / Archons**: Singular and plural, meaning "rulers." The paper primarily refers to the lower governing hierarchy established by Yaldabaoth, which participates in shaping the world, manufacturing physical bodies, and imposing cosmic bondage.

**Adam**: The primordial human of myth. The Apocryphon of John recounts the fashioning of his physical form and the descent of divine power into him as distinct narrative events.

**Luminous Epinoia / Epinoia**: The former translates to "Luminous Insight" or "Luminous Forethought." In the Apocryphon of John, she acts as the helper sent to Adam, secretly dwelling within him to guide him toward recognizing his origin and the path of return. [See English translation of the Apocryphon of John](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/apocryphonjohn.html)

## The Human Interior and Salvation

**Divine Spark**: A synthesizing term used in the paper to designate the immanent divine component within humans. It explains how individuals, even while living in the material world, remain fundamentally connected to a higher source.

**Power / Dynamis**: Meaning "power" or "capacity." Its precise referent depends on context; the paper focuses on the power derived from Sophia that entered Adam.

**Pneuma**: Commonly translated as "spirit," referring in relevant discussions to human spirituality or divine origin.

**Psyche**: Commonly translated as "soul," encompassing vital life, emotion, and cognitive intellect. It is explicitly distinguished from Pneuma in certain theological systems.

**Psychic / Pneumatic**: Adjectives related to Psyche and Pneuma respectively ("animate/soulish" vs. "spiritual"). In certain Valentinian texts, they categorize types of human beings. While Pneumatic denotes an ontological property or human classification, Divine Spark serves as the encompassing term for the immanent divine substance itself. [Human Interior and Classifications](https://iep.utm.edu/gnostic/)

**Light Fragments**: Literally "fragments of light," used in the paper to describe the image of divine luminescence dispersed and preserved within individuals.

**Heimarmene**: Meaning "Fate." In the Apocryphon of John, lower rulers utilize it to bind gods, humans, and all creation; the paper references it to parallel the dogmatic missions and preordained roles imposed by the Priests. [Relevant Passages](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/apocryphonjohn.html)

**Valentinian**: The school of thought established by Valentinus and his followers. The paper's detail regarding Sophia as "the youngest" derives from materials within this tradition.

**Duodecad**: A grouping of twelve entities. In the system recorded by Irenaeus, Sophia occupies the final position within this group of twelve. [Against Heresies I.2](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103102.htm)



## Other Religious Backgrounds Appearing in the Text

**Kabbalah**: The Jewish mystical tradition, addressing how the divine manifests and how the cosmos unfolds.

**Sefirah / Sefirot**: Singular and plural. The ten divine emanations or attributes in Kabbalah, illustrating the connection between God and the created order.

**Tree of Life**: The classical schematic organizing the Sefirot and their pathways, typically depicted as ten nodes connected by lines. [Explanation of Sefirot](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/sefirot/)

**Tetragrammaton**: Meaning "four letters," designating the divine name transliterated as YHWH. [Explanation of Name](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/6359796/jewish/The-Tetragrammaton.htm)


**Kyrie**: The vocative form of the Greek word for "Lord," widely known through the liturgical prayer Kyrie eleison ("Lord, have mercy").



## Literature and Abbreviations

**Apocryphon of John**: A text framed as secret teachings revealed by the resurrected Savior to John, recounting the transcendent realm, Sophia, Yaldabaoth, the generation of humanity, and salvation. The primary ancient text referenced in this paper, sometimes abbreviated in citations as **AJ**. [English Translation](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/apocryphonjohn.html)

**Tripartite Tractate**: A lengthy Valentinian text discussing divine origins, the formation of the world and humanity, and cosmic restoration and reconciliation. The paper cites passages concerning return and harmony. [English Translation](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/tripartite.html)

**Letter to Flora**: Ptolemy's epistle to Flora concerning the Mosaic Law and its author. It interprets the creator of the world as a just entity intermediate between the supreme Father and the adversary; cited in the paper to illustrate varying depictions of the Demiurge. [English Translation](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/flora.html)

**Against Heresies**: Irenaeus's treatise critiquing opposing doctrines, which preserves systematic descriptions of the teachings he opposed. The paper references passages concerning Valentinian Sophia. [Cited Chapter](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103102.htm)

**Nag Hammadi Library**: The collective designation for ancient codices discovered in Egypt in 1945, preserving numerous primary texts. The Nag Hammadi Library in English is the modern anthology cited in this paper.

**NHC / BG**: Manuscript citation abbreviations. NHC denotes the Nag Hammadi Codices (e.g., NHC II,1 indicates Codex II, Tractate 1). BG denotes the Berlin Codex (BG 8502,2 indicates Codex 8502, Tractate 2). Following page and line numbers provide precise textual coordinates.

**The Apocryphon of John: Synopsis of Nag Hammadi Codices II,1; III,1; and IV,1 with BG 8502,2**: The four-manuscript critical synopsis edited by Michael Waldstein and Frederik Wisse, abbreviated as **Synopsis** in the paper's citations.

**Bible / Old Testament**: The former designates Christian scripture collectively; the latter represents the portion inheriting the ancient Hebrew scriptural tradition. **Genesis** recounts creation and early humanity; **Ecclesiastes** meditates on human life, labor, and mortality; **John** is the Gospel recounting the life and ministry of Jesus; **Revelation** depicts apocalyptic visions of judgment, a new heaven, and a new earth.
