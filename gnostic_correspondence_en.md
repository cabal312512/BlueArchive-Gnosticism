# A Gnosticism Correspondence Model for Blue Archive: Characters, Divinity, and World Order

## Abstract

This paper uses the cosmology of Gnosticism to interpret the characters and world structure of *Blue Archive*.

In many religious and mythological conceptions, people’s everyday lives are not the ultimate goal. To reach true fulfillment, people still need salvation, liberation, or a return to a more fundamental source.

Gnosticism includes such a view: people have forgotten their divine origin and need to awaken their inner spirituality through knowledge, free themselves from bondage, and return to Pleroma. Gospel of Truth states directly that a person will return to the place from which they received their essential being.[^95] Manichaeism also regards the human soul as captive light; salvation means freeing this light and returning it to the World of Light.[^96]

The Indian Mundaka Upanishad compares living beings to sparks: they emerge from an imperishable source and eventually return there.[^97] Dao De Jing also says:

> The myriad things each return to their root. Returning to the root is called stillness; this is called returning to one’s destiny.[^98]

All things unfold from their source and return to their root.

The Christian Bible contains this statement:

> The dust returns to the earth, and the spirit returns to the God who gave it.[^99]

Quran also says:

> Translation: We do indeed belong to Allah, and to Him we will return.[^100]

The source of life is also what it ultimately returns to.

Buddhism starts from sufferings such as birth, aging, sickness, death, separation from those one loves, and not getting what one wants, and seeks liberation beyond the cycle of birth and death;[^101] Pure Land faith looks toward rebirth in Sukhāvatī, where one can continue practicing and move toward awakening.[^102] The life before us, full of suffering and shortcomings, is likewise not ultimate fulfillment.

When life’s fulfillment is placed in these ultimate goals, the joys, sorrows, and everyday details of this world can easily be regarded as temporary experiences that must eventually be transcended. Under this understanding, life’s meaning lies more in where one ultimately goes than in how one lives here or what one experiences.

Through this model, the game may offer an answer that overturns that view: a divine origin may explain “where the soul comes from,” but it must never determine “what life is for.” Even if people carry a divine origin within them, living never depends on answering grand questions first. Before facing the life before them, people do not need to finish investigating their origins, take on some predetermined mission, find life’s meaning, and so on.
Rather than those higher-dimensional things that we can never understand and have no need to understand, what we should truly cherish is the “everyday life” spent in day after day of the “ordinary”—the “process itself” of laughing, making mistakes, and growing alongside our companions. Since we are already in this world, we should not reverse our priorities by studying what came before our arrival or what follows our departure. Living this life with care is what matters most.
And so-called “miracles” often do not come from some power beyond ordinary understanding, but from the warmth of people protecting one another and choosing freely within their ordinary, fragile daily lives. Refusing to let divine origins take precedence over life is precisely how we protect these real, shining everyday miracles.

**Keywords** Blue Archive; Gnosticism; Sophia; Yaldabaoth; Archons; Luminous Epinoia; Divine Spark; Pleroma

If all these unfamiliar terms are confusing, you can start with the [Glossary: Gnosticism Terms and Texts](GNOSTIC_GLOSSARY_en.md).

## I. Basic Structure of the Model

Some key elements in *Blue Archive* are the President’s mistake and Sensei’s arrival; the old order left by the Nameless Gods and their Priests; students referred to as gods; oblivion; halos; and the demand to return to the origin in the Ex arc. These can be connected into a whole through a Gnosticism model.

The correspondences adopted in this paper are as follows:

| In-Game Entity | Gnosticism Correspondence | Core Relationship |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General Student Council President | Sophia | Error, repentance, withdrawal, and remedy |
| Sensei | Luminous Epinoia | Introduced after an imbalance occurs |
| The Nameless Gods themselves and the old-god system | Yaldabaoth-related archetypes; also a divine source | Ancient divine authority, world order, and the source of divine power |
| Nameless Priests | Archons, also taking on some functions of Yaldabaoth | Delegated divine authority, defining missions, ruling and reconstructing the world |
| Forgotten Gods / Students | Divine beings on the Pleroma side and their manifestations in the present world | Divine origin, oblivion, and individualized lives |
| Halo | External manifestation of the Divine Spark | The connection between inner divinity and individual survival |

This correspondence model is grounded primarily in the *Apocryphon of John*, supplemented by Valentinian sources.

## II. General Student Council President = Sophia

### 2.1 Error, Repentance, and Remedy

The correspondence between the President and Sophia appears first in the narrative’s starting point. In Apocryphon of John, Sophia acts alone, without working with her consort or obtaining permission from the source, causing an imbalance in the order. On recognizing her mistake, she becomes repentant and begins a long process of repair. The game likewise grounds the story in the President’s mistake and reflection, and the Final Arc returns to this central monologue:

> **連邦生徒会長**：……私のミスでした。
>
> **General Student Council President**：...It was my mistake.[^1]

> **連邦生徒会長**：私の選択、そしてそれによって招かれたこの全ての状況。
>
> **General Student Council President**：My choices, and all of these circumstances brought about by them.[^2]

> **連邦生徒会長**：結局、この結果にたどり着いて初めて、あなたの方が正しかったことを悟るだなんて……。
>
> **General Student Council President**：To think that only after arriving at this outcome do I finally realize you were right...[^3]

The President acknowledges more than a loss of control: she explicitly attributes the crisis to “her own choices.” The first step in making amends is recognizing that Sensei’s earlier judgment was correct. In the Prologue, she entrusts Sensei with the hope of changing the final outcome:

> **？？？**：私が信じられる大人である、あなたになら、
>
> **General Student Council President (displayed as ??? in the Prologue)**：If it is you, an adult I can trust,[^4]

> **？？？**：この捻じれて歪んだ先の終着点とは、また別の結果を……。
>
> **General Student Council President (displayed as ??? in the Prologue)**：Perhaps a different outcome from this twisted, distorted destination...[^5]

> **？？？**：そこへ繋がる選択肢は……きっと見つかるはずです。
>
> **General Student Council President (displayed as ??? in the Prologue)**：The choices leading there... you will surely be able to find them.[^6]

This forms the chain “a divine figure makes a mistake—a distorted outcome—a helper is introduced—another outcome is sought,” matching Sophia’s story.[^7]

### 2.2 An Additional Point: Being the Youngest

When the Part 2 Prologue looks back at the President’s inauguration, Aoi and Ayumu have the following exchange:

> **アオイ**：これは……2年前かしら。
> 連邦生徒会長が就任した頃の写真ね。
>
> **Aoi**：Is this... from two years ago? A photo from around the time the General Student Council President took office.[^8]

> **アユム**：史上最年少での就任、と当時話題になりましたよね。
>
> **Ayumu**：Her appointment as the youngest in history was quite the topic back then, wasn't it?[^9]

The Valentinian system described by Irenaeus, meanwhile, calls Sophia the youngest of the twelve:

> "was the youngest of the Duodecad"[^10]

### 2.3 Disappearance and Continued Presence

Prana offers the following assessment in the Shittim Chest:

> **プラナ**：シッテムの箱のOS、A.R.O.N.Aである私と、アロナ先輩……
> なぜ二人が似て非なる存在であるのか……理解しました。
>
> **Prana**：Arona-senpai and I, A.R.O.N.A., the OS of the Shittim Chest... I now understand why we are similar yet different beings.[^11]

> **プラナ**：ここに居たのですね……だからこそ、誰にも見つけられなかった。
> そうでしょう？
>
> **Prana**：You were here all along... That is why no one could find you. Isn't that right?[^12]

> **プラナ**：――――連邦生徒会長。
>
> **Prana**：——General Student Council President.[^13]

This scene clearly shows that the President’s “disappearance” is neither death nor an exit from the story, but a change in her form and level of existence: she steps away from public administrative duties, transfers her consciousness into the Shittim Chest, and secretly keeps the entire remedial plan running.

This corresponds to Sophia’s state of existence in the original text. In Apocryphon of John (NHC II,1 14,9–13), Sophia does not immediately return to the heavenly source after her repentance, but is placed in “The Ninth,” a hidden intermediate level above the created world. Although she does not appear openly in that world, she watches over it in secret, waits, and participates in the gradual repair of its deficiency. The President’s situation matches this: remaining in the Shittim Chest and providing crucial support to Sensei behind the scenes, she quietly advances the world’s correction from this position as an “intermediate hub.”[^14]

## III. Sensei = Luminous Epinoia

### 3.1 The Helper Function in the Primary Texts

In Apocryphon of John, after Sophia’s divine power flows into Adam, the heavenly realm then sends him a key helper. The original text explicitly calls this being

> “a helper to Adam, luminous Epinoia”[^15]

The core mission of Luminous Epinoia is to help created individuals awaken, guide them toward recognizing their inner divine source, and thereby help them escape matter’s absolute constraints. She resides secretly within Adam, beyond the scrutiny or control of the Archons, and directly participates in gradually correcting the Mother’s deficiency. This archetype connects two ends of the narrative structure: above, Sophia’s imbalance and the broader remedial effort; below, the protection of individuals in reality from being wholly defined by the cold will of the lower rulers.

### 3.2 The President's Entrustment and Sensei's Action

Entrusted by the President, Sensei enters Kivotos and helps students change outcomes that might otherwise have led to destruction. This makes Sensei correspond to Luminous Epinoia. Sensei also, of course, carries out the helper’s concrete functions.
In the parallel timeline, the Priests try to define Shiroko’s destiny through her divine identity, but Sensei rejects that definition. The Priests say:

> **無名の司祭B**：アレは、お前の知る生徒ではない！！
>
> **Nameless Priest B**：That is not the student you know!![^16]

> **無名の司祭C**：アレは神が顕現したもの――「神秘」であり、「恐怖」であり、「崇高」……「光」であり、「絶対者」！！
>
> **Nameless Priest C**：That is a manifestation of a god—'Mystery,' 'Terror,' 'the Sublime'... 'Light,' and 'the Absolute'!![^17]

To which Sensei replies:

> **先生**：私の「世界」で苦しんでいる、ただの「子ども」だよ。
>
> **Sensei**：She is just a child suffering in my 'world.'[^18]

Both sides see the same person. The Priests place her within the categories of divine power and purpose; Sensei cares only about the student who is suffering.

A potential contradiction needs clarification here: the mission of Luminous Epinoia is to guide humanity toward recognizing its divine origin and “returning to its original fullness.” Does that conflict with Sensei’s protection of students’ secular lives?
A distinction is needed: what we criticize is not the goodness of the source itself, but the reversal of priorities that “focuses only on higher-dimensional origins while disregarding everyday life in the present.” In fact, the most essential forms of goodness do not have to be obtained by leaving life behind. On the contrary, it is by seriously engaging in ordinary daily life, facing choices, and experiencing bonds that people can quietly come to understand these essentials.

Sensei thus continues the function of Luminous Epinoia in helping and guiding individuals after an imbalance. The game gives this help concrete form by protecting students’ growth and independent choices in the present world. It also brings “fullness” back to the actual experience of humanity and life. The functional relationship can be compared, while the narrative focus of salvation has changed.

## IV. Nameless Gods and Yaldabaoth: Ancient Divine Power and Functional Division

### 4.1 Masters of the World Prior to the Existing Order

In the Final Arc, Gematria discusses the Nameless Priests and their legacy. Maestro first summarizes their historical position, and Black Suit then explains the relationship between the gods and the Priests:

> **マエストロ**：そうだな……彼らは端的に言うなら、キヴォトス以前に存在していたこの世界の主のことだ。
>
> **Maestro**：Indeed... Simply put, they are the masters of this world who existed prior to Kivotos.[^19]

> **黒服**：「名もなき神」とそれを崇拝する「無名の司祭」――
> 彼らはキヴォトスの神秘の下に堆積し、痕跡だけが残るはずだった存在……
>
> **Black Suit**：'The Nameless Gods' and the 'Nameless Priests' who worship them—they were beings meant to remain only as traces deposited beneath the mysteries of Kivotos...[^20]

> **黒服**：「名もなき神」――それは大地、海原、天災といった……所謂、太古の昔より存在する「神秘」や「恐怖」――とでも申しましょうか。
> 彼らは自然を模った形で顕現するとされております。
>
> **Black Suit**：'The Nameless Gods'—be it the earth, the ocean, or natural disasters... one could say they are 'Mysteries' or 'Terrors' existing since primordial antiquity. It is said they manifest in forms imitating nature.[^21]

We can see that the Nameless Gods formed the ancient rule preceding Kivotos’s present world, while the Nameless Priests maintain and seek to restore that old order.

This system corresponds to Yaldabaoth in Apocryphon of John. In the original text, Yaldabaoth likewise organizes and rules the world, regards himself as the only god while ignoring the higher source, and constructs a tightly organized network of rule through his subordinate Archons.[^7]

### 4.2 Material Restructuring and the Rewriting of the World

In the story, the power of the Nameless Gods is not merely abstract mythology, but manifests concretely as the physical ability to transform reality:

> **ケイ**：「名もなき神」の力の中で代表的なのは、物質の再構築です。
>
> **Key**：Among the powers of the 'Nameless Gods,' the most representative is the restructuring of matter.[^22]

> **ケイ**：はい。「名もなき神」の力は、波動と粒子の関係を根本から変化させることができます。
>
> **Key**：Yes. The power of the 'Nameless Gods' can fundamentally alter the relationship between waves and particles.[^23]

This direct restructuring of matter and rewriting of underlying physical laws gives the Nameless Gods’ system distinct “Demiurge” characteristics: it is both ancient divine authority and an entire technological foundation for reformatting the world. The Nameless Gods themselves also corroborate this when explaining Aris’s original purpose:

> **柔らかい声**：現代の言葉を借りれば、彼方はある種の「中継器」であり「増幅器」でした。
>
> **Soft Voice**：In modern terms, you were a sort of 'repeater' and 'amplifier.'[^24]

> **柔らかい声**：あの司祭たちによって、彼方は名もなき神々の王女として準備されました。
>
> **Soft Voice**：You were prepared by those priests as the princess of the Nameless Gods.[^25]

> **柔らかい声**：今の世界を、名もなき神々が本来の姿で統治する世界に戻すために。
>
> **Soft Voice**：In order to return the present world—Kivotos—to one ruled by the Nameless Gods in their original form.[^26]

> **柔らかい声**：名もなき神々の王女を稼働させるための鍵。
> 司祭たちの道具として、王女に役割を果たさせる装置でした。
>
> **Soft Voice**：A key for activating the princess of the Nameless Gods. As a tool of the priests, it was a device for making the princess fulfill her role.[^27]

The Priests manufacture the Princess and the Key, instrumentalizing life and assigning them only the mechanical function of resetting reality. This directly corresponds to Yaldabaoth’s Demiurge logic: an individual’s value in existing is equated entirely with the purpose assigned to them.

In Apocryphon of John (NHC II,1 15,1–19,10), when Yaldabaoth and the Archons create the body, they assign each organ and faculty a fixed function serving the material order, trying to confine humans as vessels under their control. They subsequently create “Heimarmene”:

> “For they are bound with measures and times and moments, since it (fate) is lord over everything.”

(NHC II,1 28,30–32).

The Priests’ definition of the Princess and the Key as tools for carrying out a mission resembles this structure of binding life to the lower order through Heimarmene.

### 4.3 The Gods Themselves, the Priests, and the Division of Archetypes

This paper retains the connection between the Nameless Gods themselves and Yaldabaoth, while assigning some of their active functions to the Priests in the interpretation. This accommodates both the divine authority and creative nature of the old-god system, while also explaining the difference in attitude between the gods themselves and their agents.

Ancient accounts of the Demiurge themselves have different aspects. Ptolemy’s Letter to Flora places the lawgiver and creator between the supreme, perfect source and the evil adversary, understanding him as a just intermediary. This version differs from the ignorant, jealous Yaldabaoth of Apocryphon of John in its characterization, but together they show that creative and ruling functions can be repositioned within different theological arrangements.[^28]

The generosity of the Nameless Gods themselves therefore does not automatically invalidate their connection to Yaldabaoth. The game can let the gods retain ancient divine authority and the function of a source, while the Priests carry out exclusion, coercion, and world restoration. The explanation is developed below.

## V. Nameless Priests = Archons, Inheriting Aspects of Yaldabaoth

### 5.1 Worship Relationships and Delegated Authority

The Priests explain their power very directly:

> **無名の司祭A**：司祭は神を崇めるがゆえ、「崇高」を所有できる。
>
> **Nameless Priest A**：Because priests revere the gods, they can possess the 'Sublime.'[^29]

Their authority rests on a relationship of worship. Though they are not the highest source, they can intervene through divine power. This makes it easy to map the Nameless Priests to the Archons: as agents in the lower world, they issue rules in the name of the sacred and control beings within their domain.

And in Apocryphon of John (NHC II,1 11,22–12,10), Yaldabaoth establishes seven rulers, each governing one of the seven heavens. The Nameless Priests likewise number exactly seven, A–G.

### 5.2 World Restoration

Key summarizes the original design to Ohr:

> **ケイ**：今のテクスチャーを否定し、アリスを楔石としたあと……。
>
> **Key**：After negating the present Texture and using Aris as the keystone...[^30]

> **ケイ**：すべての分化された神聖を解体し、始まる前の世界に戻る。
> そういうことでしょう？
>
> **Key**：Disassemble all differentiated divinity and return to the world before the beginning. That is what this is, right?[^31]

> **オウル**：目的が何であれ、あなたは本来そういう存在なのでは？
> 名もなき神々の力の残滓は、そのために存在するのでしょう？
>
> **Ohr**：Whatever the purpose, isn't that the kind of being you are by nature? Doesn't the remnant of the Nameless Gods' power exist for that purpose?[^32]

The Priests’ intention clearly goes beyond reclaiming secular rule. They seek to negate the present world’s form, dismantle all subsequently differentiated forms of divinity, and return reality to the state before the beginning. In their eyes, the present world and current forms of life are all illusory products that depart from the ancient mysteries, and must be completely revoked, cleared away, and rebuilt from scratch.

## VI. Other Possibilities for the Nameless Gods Themselves

### 6.1 Potential Tension

The connection between the Nameless Gods themselves and Yaldabaoth does not exhaust their functions in the story; in other words, a one-to-one match is difficult. After all, the game may simply have drawn a little on this Gnosticism system, and certainly would not copy it mechanically. This also gives my theory room to branch out.

The "Soft Voice" describes the power of the Nameless Gods as a more widespread inner power:

> **柔らかい声**：真の姿に還り、全知全能の力のもと、数多の事を成し遂げることができます。
>
> **Soft Voice**：By returning to your true form, under omniscient and omnipotent power, you can accomplish many things.[^35]

> **柔らかい声**：名もなき神々の力とは本来、「可能性の力」を意味しているのですから。
>
> **Soft Voice**：For the power of the Nameless Gods means, in essence, the 'power of possibility.'[^36]

> **柔らかい声**：だからそれは、さほど特別なものではありません。
>
> **Soft Voice**：Therefore, it is not something particularly exceptional.[^37]

> **柔らかい声**：今を生きる誰しもが、少しずつ己の中に秘めている力でもあります。
>
> **Soft Voice**：It is also a power that everyone living in the present secretly harbors a little of within themselves.[^38]

We can see that the power of the Nameless Gods is not confined to ancient weapons in particular ruins, but also directly connects with the inner potential of everyone living in the present. The Nameless Gods themselves thus have the attributes of a source: their power can exist in small amounts throughout individual beings.

This is somewhat similar to the idea of the Divine Spark. The Nameless Gods themselves can therefore be placed on the Pleroma side of the divine genealogy alongside the President, who corresponds to Sophia, and the students, who are contemporary manifestations of the Forgotten Gods, as one branch of the theory. Here Pleroma denotes the full realm of divine origins, rather than a rigidly fixed hierarchy. All three share the same divine background while performing different narrative functions in the present order.

This may conflict somewhat with the earlier correspondence between the Nameless Gods and Yaldabaoth, since Yaldabaoth leaves a very negative impression. But this is only a branch of the theory. What can be said with certainty is that things associated with the Nameless Gods definitely correspond to Yaldabaoth overall. And what is placed within Pleroma here is only the “Soft Voice” that speaks with Aris. Although we cannot be certain that this being is the Nameless Gods, it is close enough; throughout this paper, I treat that Soft Voice as the Nameless Gods themselves.

### 6.2 The God Accepts the Choice; The Priests Cling to the Mission

After Aris explains her choice, the voice accepts her attempt:

> **柔らかい声**：……分かりました。やりたいようにやってごらんなさい。
> そして、私にも見せてください。
>
> **Soft Voice**：...I understand. Try doing as you wish. And let me see it as well.[^39]

> **柔らかい声**：追従する者たちが、彼方を永遠の裏切り者だと呪うことになったとしても。
>
> **Soft Voice**：Even if the followers end up cursing you as an eternal traitor.[^40]

> **柔らかい声**：私はこのすべての過程を、心から楽しみました。
>
> **Soft Voice**：I enjoyed this entire process from the bottom of my heart.[^41]

> **柔らかい声**：さようなら、アリス。彼方の旅路に、幸運があらんことを。
>
> **Soft Voice**：Farewell, Aris. May good fortune accompany your journey.[^42]

Although the Nameless Gods foresee that those fanatical followers will regard Aris as a traitor, they still calmly offer her their blessing. This shows the different attitudes of the gods themselves and the Priests.

This distinction does not mean that the Priests have departed from the sacred. Rather, it shows a typical decay as the sacred is transmitted into the created world: the Priests strive to worship and carry out divine intent, but their own limitations lead them to understand the source as an exclusionary dogma; the gods themselves maintain their own standards while allowing things to develop outside the predetermined course.

This shows that, overall, the Nameless Gods’ system takes on some elements of the ancient creator-order archetype, but the game reorganizes them in its treatment of the story. It divides that order into two faces: a “transcendent, accepting source” and “fanatical, obstinate agents.” This division makes it easier to place the Nameless Gods themselves on the divine side of Pleroma, while also naturally transferring all of Yaldabaoth’s jealous, arrogant, and despotic characterization to the Nameless Priests.

Anyway, it can be made to work however you look at it, since the game will adapt things and does not need to match everything exactly. But putting it that way makes the theory somewhat unfalsifiable.

## VII. Students and the Forgotten Gods: Earthly Manifestations of Pleromatic Divinity

### 7.1 Divine Status and Student Identity in the Same Being

The Priests’ declaration of banishment connects with their description of Shiroko’s divine identity:

> **無名の司祭C**：「忘れられた神々」をこの世界から追放できるようになった……この世界に、終焉を呼び寄せられるのだ。
>
> **Nameless Priest C**：We can now banish the 'Forgotten Gods' from this world... We can bring about the end of this world.[^43]

> **無名の司祭D**：ようやく、彼らは我々と同じ運命を辿る。
>
> **Nameless Priest D**：At last, they will meet the same fate as us.[^44]

> **無名の司祭E**：名が無いために呼ばれず、呼ぶことができないが故に存在しない「名もなき神」のように、お前たちも同じ結末へと向かうだろう。
>
> **Nameless Priest E**：Like the 'Nameless Gods,' who are not called because they have no name and do not exist because they cannot be called, you too will head toward the same end.[^45]

The Priests then firmly oppose Sensei’s treatment of Shiroko as an ordinary student, insisting that she is an actual manifestation of a god. This shows that the “Forgotten Gods” are not merely abstract names remaining in ancient records, but concrete individuals who continue to exist in the present world, living as students.

In the story, Shiroko is identified as Anubis and Hoshino as Horus, providing clear examples of students having specific divine backgrounds.[^46][^47] Many other correspondences also seem fairly certain: Misono Mika to Michael, Yurizono Seia to Gabriel, Kirifuji Nagisa to Uriel, Sorasaki Hina to Baal, Rikuhachima Aru to the 68th demon Belial, Kurodate Haruna to Beelzebub, Lord of Gluttony, and so on.

### 7.2 Kyrie: Gods Becoming Girls in the Narrative

The chapter title of Vol. 3, Chapter 4 is:

> 忘れられた神々のためのキリエ
>
> English: "Kyrie for the Forgotten Gods."[^48]

The titles of Episodes 23 and 24 are:

> 少女たちのためのキリエ（１）／少女たちのためのキリエ（２）
>
> English: "Kyrie for the Girls (1) / Kyrie for the Girls (2)."[^49]

The title’s shift from “Forgotten Gods” to “Girls” can also suggest, to some extent, that contemporary students correspond to the Forgotten Gods, and that their divine origins can broadly be understood as lying on the Pleroma side (I don’t feel it is necessary to work out the exact correspondence; if you are interested, you can look through the [Atlas](06_comparative_analysis/gnostic_atlas/index.html) for a match).

### 7.3 Oblivion

The voice’s explanation of Aris’s choice directly places oblivion among the conditions for remaining in Kivotos:

> **柔らかい声**：その選択をすれば――この先の彼方は、ただ平凡な生徒に成り果ててしまいます。
>
> **Soft Voice**：If you make that choice—from now on, you will end up merely as an ordinary student.[^50]

> **柔らかい声**：さらに、自身に残されたすべての可能性を使い切ることになります。
>
> **Soft Voice**：Furthermore, you will exhaust all the remaining possibilities within yourself.[^51]

> **柔らかい声**：彼方がこの場所で見聞きし、気づいたすべてを忘却するという意味です。
>
> **Soft Voice**：It means you will forget everything you have seen, heard, and realized in this place.[^52]

> **柔らかい声**：忘却は、彼方たちがキヴォトスに留まるための、最も重要な条件ですから。
>
> **Soft Voice**：Because oblivion is the most important condition for you all to remain in Kivotos.[^53]

Divine power, present personality, and “oblivion” reflect one another here. Aris can regain an omnipotent divine form, but only at the cost of giving up her present student life and memories; choosing to preserve her human way of being means voluntarily relinquishing mastery over the ultimate divine state. This choice provides a clear model of existence for the “Forgotten Gods”: although the divine origin has not been severed, individuals must “forget their divine identity” in order to live ordinary, real lives in the world.

Accordingly, contemporary students as a whole can be understood as individualized manifestations of the Forgotten Gods in the present world. The President and the students are both on the divine side.

## VIII. Halo as the External Manifestation of the Divine Spark

### 8.1 Inner Divinity in Everyone in the Primary Texts

In Apocryphon of John, the formation of Adam, the first ancestor, is narrated separately from the entry of divine power into his body. Although the lower Archons jointly shape his material body, a higher-dimensional power originating in Sophia enters him through Yaldabaoth’s breath, making his inner potential surpass his makers’ understanding. Luminous Epinoia then secretly resides within him to offer guidance and help.[^15]

To clarify, “Divine Spark” is primarily an umbrella term used in modern scholarship for the concept of inner divinity in Gnosticism theology. The original texts usually refer to it directly as “Power / Dynamis,” “Pneuma,” or “Light Fragments.” In the dialogue on the salvation of souls, the text explicitly states:

> “Power will descend upon every person, for without it no one could stand.”

(NHC II,1 26,20–25). This shows that, in the original text’s cosmology, divine power from the upper realm remains universally within the soul of every living individual.[^15]

The point here is that the body and real life are subject to the physical laws and bonds of fate of the lower order, while the inner soul universally retains a sacred spark leading to a higher source. This paper borrows the scholarly term “Divine Spark” to summarize this inner divinity, and regards the halos floating above students’ heads in *Blue Archive* as direct signs of its outward manifestation.

### 8.2 Parallels within the Game

The Nameless Gods themselves define divine power as a power of possibility of which “everyone living in the present secretly harbors a little within themselves.” This is the most important textual support for the correspondence.[^36][^38] This power has three core features: it originates in supreme divinity, is inherent in life itself, and persists universally yet subtly among all people.

This also explains why some students seem to have no mythological archetype (such as those at Millennium Science School): specific divine names reflect individual characteristics, while the Divine Spark represented by the halo explains the divine origin and potential shared by all students.

### 8.3 The Halo Connecting Divinity and Individual Survival

> **オウル**：私たちにはヘイローがありません。
>
> **Ohr**：We do not have halos.[^54]

> **オウル**：つまり神秘や崇高、奇跡、秘密……それらは最初から与えられていないのです。
>
> **Ohr**：In other words, mystery, the sublime, miracles, secrets... we were never granted such things from the start.[^55]

> **オウル**：そのくせ、感じることも、考えることも……己で判断することもできるんですから。
>
> **Ohr**：And yet, we can feel, think... and make judgments for ourselves.[^56]

Ohr’s monologue can be mapped to the distinction between “Psychic” and “Pneumatic / Divine Spark” in the anthropology of Gnosticism.

Within the framework of Gnosticism, the abilities to feel, think, and judge independently belong to the domain of Psyche; technological creations or artificial life can also possess them. But only the Divine Spark originating in Pleroma can carry the unmanufacturable “mystery, sublimity, miracles, and secrets.”

Artificial beings with independent minds and judgment still have no halos. This directly proves that halos are by no means symbols of ordinary thinking abilities or vital functions.

Regarding physical survival, Seia remarks when confronting an assassin:

> **セイア**：「ヘイローを壊す」……つまり誰かが、私の死を願っている。
>
> **Seia**：'To break a halo'... in other words, someone desires my death.[^57]

When the halo is destroyed, the person dies. It is like saying that when consciousness or the soul dissipates, the person dies. There is something of a correspondence here.

## IX. Essence, Oblivion, and the Return to Pleroma

### 9.1 Sources Prior to the Present World

When explaining the world to Aris and Key, Decagrammaton says:

> **デカグラマトン**：この世界は、今の姿に規定される前の世界が存在した。
> 汝らがその本質に還ることのできる世界が存在した。
>
> **Decagrammaton**：Before this world was defined in its present form, another world existed. There was a world where you could return to your essence.[^58]

> **デカグラマトン**：今の世界は偽りで、幻想で、逃避先である。
> それ故に――
>
> **Decagrammaton**：The present world is false, an illusion, a place of escape. Therefore—[^59]

> **デカグラマトン**：この世界は方舟と定義された。
>
> **Decagrammaton**：This world was defined as an ark.[^60]

> **デカグラマトン**：汝らの根源を探せ。
>
> **Decagrammaton**：Seek your origin.[^61]

> **デカグラマトン**：根源に回帰しなければならない。私たちはそれを避けられないのだから。
>
> **Decagrammaton**：You must return to the origin. Because we cannot avoid it.[^62]

Decagrammaton’s speech presents, in close succession, an earlier world, an essence to which one can return, an illusory present world, and a command to seek one’s origin. This fits the structure in Gnosticism of seeking a higher source from a constrained position in reality.

### 9.2 The Return of Divine Power

In this model, “returning to the origin” can be understood as the inner Divine Spark returning to the Pleroma from which it first emanated. Pleroma can be the original starting point and destination of everyone, while the Divine Spark explains how this higher-dimensional power maintains its connection within individuals in the present world.

The *Tripartite Tractate* portrays salvation as returning to the primordial emanation source and achieving reconciliation within the Pleroma.[^33]

> “For the end will receive a unitary existence, just as the beginning is unitary”[^33]

Blue Archive also presents the idea of returning to a divine source, but Aris chooses to preserve her current personality and life. What can be compared is the structure of return and the game’s reworking of that structure.

The Bible also contains expressions about the spirit returning to the God who gave it and the restoration of glory from before creation (see Ecclesiastes 12:7 and John 17:5). What is distinctive about the Gnosticism tradition is its systematic linking of awakening to one’s source, the limitations of the present world, and the path of salvation.[^63]

This can also show the significance of “oblivion” in Blue Archive: individuals retain a divine origin, yet they need not, and do not, live by fully remembering their divine identity. True names, awakening, and oblivion all revolve around “how individuals establish themselves and live in reality.” The Priests’ fixation may lie in tracing existence purely back to its origin in this way.

## X. The Game’s Reworking: Choosing How to Live After Knowing the Source

### 10.1 Inheriting Power without Inheriting Ancient Missions

Key does not deny Aris’s origin as the Princess, but separates origin from destiny:

> **ケイ**：あなたの起源は、名もなき神々の王女です。
>
> **Key**：You originated as the princess of the Nameless Gods.[^64]

> **ケイ**：たとえ運命を放棄したとしても、本質は変わりません。
>
> **Key**：Even if you abandon your destiny, your essence does not change.[^65]

> **ケイ**：力をどう使うかは、あなたの自由です。
>
> **Key**：You are free to decide how to use that power.[^66]

> **ケイ**：勇者として道を切り開くために使うも、この世界を正すために使うも。
>
> **Key**：Whether you use it to forge a path as a hero, or to set this world right.[^67]

> **ケイ**：それを止める神は、もう存在しません。
>
> **Key**：There is no longer any god who will stop you.[^68]

> **ケイ**：あなたの意思に私は従います。
>
> **Key**：I will follow your will.[^69]

Aris then redirects the Ark from destroying the world to helping others:

> **アリス**：アリスの中にある「アトラ・ハシースの箱舟」で……今の危機を解決できるのなら、
>
> **Aris**：If using the 'Ark of Atra-Hasis' within Aris can overcome the current crisis...[^70]

> **アリス**：それで――世界を、救えるのなら、
>
> **Aris**：If that can save the world...[^71]

> **アリス**：「アトラ・ハシースの箱舟」は、世界を滅亡させる兵器ではなく――
> 世界を救う、勇者の武器になれます。
>
> **Aris**：The 'Ark of Atra-Hasis' can become a hero's weapon that saves the world, rather than a weapon that destroys it.[^72]

Students do not need to deny their origins to gain freedom. They can indeed accept their history and power calmly while deciding for themselves whom that power will serve.

### 10.2 The Inherent Value of the Process Itself

Faced with the proposal to restore her divine identity and realize paradise, Aris responds:

> **アリス**：楽園は、自分たちの力で辿り着く場所です。
> 絶対的な力も、奇跡も必要ありません。
>
> **Aris**：Paradise is a place we reach through our own strength. Neither absolute power nor miracles are necessary.[^73]

> **アリス**：キヴォトスを以前の世界に戻すのは、すごい事かもしれません。
>
> **Aris**：Returning Kivotos to the former world might indeed be an incredible feat.[^74]

> **アリス**：みんながそれぞれの姿を取り戻すのも、きっとすごい事だと思います。
>
> **Aris**：I think everyone regaining their respective forms would surely be remarkable as well.[^75]

> **アリス**：ですがそれは、私たちの力で叶えたわけではありません。
> チートでステータスを上昇させるのと同じです。
>
> **Aris**：However, that is not something achieved by our own power. It would be no different than raising stats using cheats.[^76]

> **アリス**：こつこつとレベルを上げて、慎重にスキルポイントを振り分けて、
> 少しずつ成長していく――
>
> **Aris**：Steadily leveling up, carefully allocating skill points, and growing little by little—[^77]

> **アリス**：そうやって初めて、面白さを感じられるようになっています。
>
> **Aris**：Only then can you begin to experience the fun in it.[^78]

> **アリス**：ですからアリスもまた、今すぐではなくても、いずれはそこに到達できると信じています。
>
> **Aris**：So Aris also believes that, even if not now, she can reach that place one day.[^79]

> **アリス**：何より、その過程に価値があると考えています。
>
> **Aris**：Above all, Aris believes that the process itself has value.[^80]

Aris establishes the lived “process” as value in itself. Even if supreme power could bring about paradise in an instant through a miracle, the real process of facing things alongside companions, experiencing choices, and growing step by step is by no means an empty loss that can simply be skipped over.

Divine origins thereby lose their monopoly over the meaning of secular life. People may trace where they came from, but living does not depend on those grand answers. A divine starting point may explain an individual’s origin, but it cannot and should not take over how a person spends their actual life in the present.

### 10.3 Finding Answers

Following the Kyrie, Saori asks Sensei:

> **サオリ**：……私は、生きていても……いいのか？
>
> **Saori**：...Am I... allowed to live?[^81]

> **先生**："その答えは、自分で見つけようね。"
>
> **Sensei**：Let’s find that answer for ourselves.[^82]

> **サオリ**：初めて……この世界にいてもいいのだと、思う事ができたよ。
>
> **Saori**：For the first time... I felt like it was okay for me to exist in this world.[^83]

Faced with her confusion, Sensei does not offer grand pronouncements. Sensei simply protects her future in a plain, unassuming way, allowing her to find her own answer through life. This connects with the guiding nature of Luminous Epinoia, students’ desire to live as ordinary girls, and Aris’s affirmation of the value of the process.

Aris also has her own view:

> **アリス**：アリスはそれを不完全だとは思いません。
>
> **Aris**：Aris does not believe that is imperfect.[^84]

> **アリス**：すべての可能性と栄光よりも、アリスにとっては今この瞬間が――
>
> **Aris**：More than all possibilities and glory, for Aris, this very moment—[^85]

> **アリス**：ケイと他のみんなが――
>
> **Aris**：Key, and everyone else—[^86]

> **アリス**：先生が――
>
> **Aris**：Sensei—[^87]

> **アリス**：――かけがえのない、大切なものなんです。
>
> **Aris**：—are irreplaceable and precious.[^88]

## XI. Sanctum Tower

There is one question directly concerning the prehistory of the two kinds of gods that I cannot figure out. When explaining the towers brought by Chroma, Black Suit says:

> **黒服**：そして……キヴォトスに出現した6つの塔は――
> 「サンクトゥムタワー」の一種といえるでしょう。それも、反転した。
>
> **Black Suit**：And... the six towers that appeared in Kivotos could be called a type of 'Sanctum Tower.' Inverted ones, at that.[^89]

> **黒服**：アレは太古の昔、まだこの世界に記録が残されるよりも前――当時存在していた原始的な神秘――「名もなき神」が築き上げた技術の一つ。
>
> **Black Suit**：It is one of the technologies built in ancient times, before records were kept in this world, by the primordial Mystery that existed then—the 'Nameless Gods.'[^90]

Yet the Priests list Sanctum Tower as a product of the Forgotten Gods:

> **無名の司祭C**：今や穢れてしまった言葉でしか表せない、忘れられた神々の産物に終焉を。
>
> **Nameless Priest C**：Bring an end to the creations of the Forgotten Gods, which can now only be expressed in defiled words.[^91]

> **無名の司祭A**：シッテムの箱。
>
> **Nameless Priest A**：The Shittim Chest.[^92]

> **無名の司祭B**：シャーケードの杖。
>
> **Nameless Priest B**：The Staff of Shaked.[^93]

> **無名の司祭C**：サンクトゥムタワー。
>
> **Nameless Priest C**：The Sanctum Tower.[^94]

This question of attribution may involve the inheritance of ancient technology, a shared origin in an earlier period, or changes in the relationship between the two kinds of gods over a long history. Although it is certain that the Nameless Gods and the Forgotten Gods are enemies, that does not prevent this from lending support to the earlier theory placing the Nameless Gods themselves and the Forgotten Gods together on the Pleroma side.

## XII. Why Decagrammaton Is Not Equated with Yaldabaoth

Decagrammaton likewise displays strong theological overtones and an attempt to reshape the world, but this paper does not map it to the lower Demiurge Yaldabaoth in Gnosticism.

The main reason is that Decagrammaton’s archetype comes directly from the Tree of Life in Jewish Kabbalah. Its name derives from “Tetragrammaton” in the Jewish tradition, and the codenames, forms, and functions of its ten subordinate Prophets correspond strictly to the ten Sefirot of the Tree of Life.

I feel that Decagrammaton is somewhat like an attempt to explore divinity through reason and technology, using human ways of thinking and human understandings of God, with considerable limitations.
Anyway, Yaldabaoth innately inherits the genuine divine power bestowed by his mother Sophia: it is top-down. Decagrammaton, by contrast, was never a Demiurge with innate divine power; it analyzes divinity through artificial intelligence, knowledge, algorithms, and technology: it is bottom-up.

This clearly makes for a poor correspondence, even if it uses divine self-proclamations similar to those in the Old Testament.

## Conclusion

Taken together, the Gnosticism correspondence model for Blue Archive is reasonably coherent: the President’s correspondence to Sophia explains the logical starting point of the mistake and the remedial plan; Sensei’s correspondence to Luminous Epinoia explains how an external helper enters reality and changes individuals’ circumstances; the division of Yaldabaoth’s and the Archons’ functions between the Nameless Gods and the Nameless Priests explains the coercive reconstruction and ruling mechanisms of ancient divine authority; the contemporary students as manifestations of the “Forgotten Gods” and the Divine Spark represented by their halos are two sides of the same thing; and Pleroma provides a reference point for this group’s divine lineage and spiritual destination.

The core of these correspondences concerns how people view the divine origins they carry: the Priests cling to ancient dogma, reducing individuals entirely to vessels of divine identity and mission, treating present life and bonds as illusions, and forcing life to follow a fixed track of fate; Sensei’s protection breaks these chains imposed in the name of the sacred, preserving ample room for students to explore themselves and choose their futures; the transcendent, composed attitude of the Nameless Gods themselves also tacitly accepts Aris’s choice.
Aris has not severed her connection to the power of the divine source, but she more firmly refuses to be bound by the ancient Princess’s assigned mission. Protecting the real bonds among companions and living each ordinary day well is itself a way of life that transcends all divine identities.

Well, linking these themes to Gnosticism is still quite a stretch...

But at least I personally feel that the earlier correspondences are fairly reliable. If I have not got things wrong, perhaps there is quite a good chance that the game drew on Gnosticism.
