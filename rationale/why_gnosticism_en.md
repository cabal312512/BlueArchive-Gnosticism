# Why Gnosticism Came to Mind

I connect *Blue Archive* with Gnosticism because the game already draws repeatedly on religion, mythology, and esotericism, so adding one more to the list does not seem like a problem.

## Arks and Sacred Objects

- **Kivotos** corresponds to the Greek κιβωτός, meaning a box or chest, also used in the Bible for Noah's ark and the Ark of the Covenant. **Shittim Chest** points to the acacia wood used to make the Ark in Exodus 25.
- Shaked in the **Rod of Shaked** means almond tree, suggesting Aaron's Rod, which buds, blossoms, and bears almonds in Numbers 17. Hebrews 9:4 also lists this rod among the contents of the Ark. In the game, the device takes the form of a smartwatch.
- **Perez-Uzzah** is a process name in the Shittim Chest. It is also the name given to the place where Uzzah was struck dead by God after touching the Ark in 2 Samuel 6.
- The **ship of Utnapishtim** and the **Ark of Atra-Hasis** borrow the names of flood survivors from the *Epic of Gilgamesh* and *Atrahasis*, respectively.
- Sanctum in **Sanctum Tower** comes directly from the Latin Sanctum Sanctorum, the Holy of Holies—the most sacred space in the Temple, where the Ark of the Covenant was kept.

## Church History, Scripture, and Liturgy

- **Trinity, Arius, and the First Council** correspond to the Council of Nicaea in 325 and the Arian controversy.
- **Communio Sanctorum**, used for the game's saint system, is also the communion of saints in the Apostles' Creed. **Vanitas vanitatum et omnia vanitas** comes from the Latin translation of Ecclesiastes 1:2: “Vanity of vanities; all is vanity.”
- **Kyrie eleison** means “Lord, have mercy” and is a Christian liturgical prayer. The **Angels of the Apocalypse** also explicitly connect with the trumpet-bearing angels of Revelation.

## Names and Structures from Kabbalah

- The name **Gematria** comes directly from Hebrew Kabbalistic letter numerology, Gematria.
- In the game, **Decagrammaton** is glossed as a divine name of ten letters, a naming pattern that can be compared with Tetragrammaton, the four-letter divine name.
- The ten Prophets correspond to the ten Sephirot of the Kabbalistic Tree of Life: Kether, Chokhmah, Binah, Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, Yesod, and Malkuth. There is also the hidden sefirah Da'at.
- **Ain, Soph, and Aur** together form Ain Soph Aur, “limitless light.” Western Hermetic Qabalah also presents Ain, Ain Soph, and Ain Soph Aur as three levels preceding manifestation.

## The Students' Mythological Prototypes

The schools and students also display clusters of mythological imagery.

- **Abydos** was an important religious center in ancient Egypt. Alongside Anubis and Horus, the students' designs can also be compared with Bastet, Hathor, and Thoth.
- **Gehenna** is a biblical term for hell. Student prototypes can be compared with figures from the Goetia and wider demonological traditions, including Baal, Belial, Astaroth, and Beelzebub.
- **Trinity**'s angelic and saintly imagery can be connected with angels such as Michael, Gabriel, and Uriel and with traditions of martyrdom.
- **Hyakkiyako** draws on Japanese supernatural beings, shrines, and onmyōdō imagery; **Shanhaijing** features Chinese mythical creatures and alchemy.
